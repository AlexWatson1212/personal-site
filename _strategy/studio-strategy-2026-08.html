01

Executive verdict

Choose Your Practice Website is the right entry point, and it is commercially stronger than a clarity-first bespoke offer for three testable reasons. It is the only route you can sell without a sales call. It converts your largest weakness — no third-party clients — into your main asset, eight complete directions a buyer can look at and judge. And no UK studio occupies the slot: the template shops in this niche are American Squarespace sellers at $179–$399, and the UK specialists are either £16–£45/month subscriptions or quote-only agencies with no published prices.

Three things in the current plan are wrong.

£495 is too low. It sits below a five-page Wix build at £695 and signals the opposite of what you are selling. Two tiers, at £950 and £2,450. Delete the middle one.

Twelve directions before a single paying client is overbuilding. You have eight. Finish six to an unarguable standard and stop. Add one per two websites sold, driven by what buyers ask for and cannot find.

You are one platform, not two. Build on your existing stack and add a small editing layer for the four or five fields that actually change — fees, availability, bio, a note. Squarespace as a second tier would double your QA surface, halve your differentiation, and place you beside designers charging £1,700–£4,000 for the same platform.

The binding constraint is not systems, directions or software. It is that nobody has paid you yet. Everything else here is secondary to three named, credited clients before December. Your cohort, placement colleagues and supervisor are the fastest honest route, and you have already said you are comfortable marketing there.

Decide the price on Monday. Send twenty messages by Friday.

Four things this tells you

  • There is a hole in the middle. The UK market is a barbell: cheap subscriptions at £16–£45/month, cheap one-offs at £245–£999, and quote-only agencies. Between "templated Wix build" and "£2,000+ custom project" there is almost nobody, and nobody at all doing it with a curated, browsable, therapist-specific catalogue. That is your slot. Judgement
  • Pricing opacity is the sector's weakest point. Five of the UK providers found publish no prices whatsoever. For a buyer who is already anxious about being sold to — which is precisely the therapist temperament — a clear published price is not just convenience, it is a trust signal. You already do this. Say why you do it. Judgement
  • Subscription is the incumbent model, and it is your best contrast. £30/month for ten years is £3,600 and you own nothing. A one-off fee plus an optional care plan you can leave is a genuinely better deal, and it is easy to say plainly.
  • The US template shops prove the catalogue model works, and have not crossed the Atlantic. Hold Space Creative describes itself as "the original Squarespace template shop dedicated specifically to therapists & coaches". No UK equivalent was found. Verified

What is actually wrong with therapist websites

From two specialist critiques with named, observed defects rather than generic UX advice (Design for Therapists, High Five Design): a live site with an entirely empty footer; a Wix watermark still in the header; verbatim textbook passages describing modalities; a Bitmoji used as the professional headshot; fees omitted because the therapist fears deterring people, which "often creates more hesitation" instead; specialisms buried inside long About paragraphs; group practices forcing visitors to click through every therapist's bio to find the right one; and — my favourite, because it is so specific — "your website should reflect you, not the Brené fan club." Verified

Note what is not on that list: bad taste. The failures are structural and editorial — missing information, buried decisions, borrowed language. Your instinct that generic marketing feels uncomfortable to therapists is right, but the commercial opportunity is slightly different from the aesthetic one. Most therapist websites are not ugly. They are unusable at the moment of decision. Judgement

What therapy clients appear to value — the strongest evidence available

The single most useful research finding for this business

Jane May's 2018 doctoral thesis at the University of Brighton, What factors influence a client's choice of counsellor or psychotherapist in a private practice setting?, found that third-party recommendation was the primary selection factor; where that was unavailable, clients searched online for location, experience and perceived relatability. Critically: clients "assessed these factors using the photograph and rhetoric on the therapist's website or directory entry", relying substantially on "unconscious projection and 'gut instinct'" — with qualifications, professional standing and modality given comparatively little weight.

Verified University of Brighton research repository

This is UK-specific, on-point doctoral research that says the two things your studio actually sells — the photograph and the words — are the primary drivers of an emotionally-led decision, ahead of credentials. It is corroborated experimentally by a 2025 Psychotherapy Research paper finding facial attractiveness independently raises the likelihood of a psychotherapist being chosen, separate from perceived trustworthiness (DOI 10.1080/10503307.2025.2494272; title and journal verified, full text paywalled).

Two consequences. First, photography is not a nice-to-have add-on, it is close to the core product, and almost every therapist has a bad photo. Second, you can say this to a prospective client with evidence behind it, which is a much better sales conversation than "I have good taste."

General web-credibility research supports the same direction without being specific to therapy: Lindgaard et al. (2006) put visual-appeal formation at roughly 50 milliseconds, correlating with later credibility judgements; the Stanford Web Credibility guidelines (2002, foundational rather than current) list real contact details, displayed credentials, professional design, visible maintenance and error-free presentation as trust factors. Both are well-established; neither is new. Verified

Two evidence gaps you should know about

Extensive searching found no published data on what proportion of UK therapists have their own website, and no credible survey of where UK therapists actually get clients from — the organic-search versus directory versus word-of-mouth split. These are the two most decision-relevant numbers for your business and they do not exist publicly. Do not let anyone, including me, quote them at you. You can generate the first yourself in an afternoon: sample 150 Counselling Directory profiles in three regions and count outbound website links. That is real proprietary evidence, and it is also a publishable article. Judgement

Recommended business model

One recommendation. A curated catalogue as the front door, one technical platform, two commercial tiers, and a scarcity rule that makes the word "template" untrue rather than merely unwelcome.

The catalogue is the entry point. The bespoke route is the ceiling, not the door.

Five reasons, in order of weight:

  1. It is the only offer you can sell without a call. You have said you can sustain twenty to thirty minutes a day on marketing and will not become a loud online personality. An enquiry-led bespoke offer requires a sales conversation for every single unit. That is not a marketing constraint, it is a capacity ceiling — and it caps you at whatever number of consultations you can bear per month.
  2. It solves your credibility problem instead of exposing it. You have no third-party client work. A bespoke-first studio asks a buyer to trust a process they cannot see. A catalogue asks them to look at eight finished things. The evidence in §2 says buyers in this market decide on what they can see and feel, not on stated process.
  3. Clarity-first has a positioning problem you have already sensed. "Get clear before you get a website" implies the buyer is unclear. Therapists are trained in self-reflection and many will find that faintly insulting. As an optional deeper route it reads as generous; as the required front door it reads as a gate.
  4. The slot is empty in the UK. See §2. The nearest comparable products are American Squarespace templates at $179–$399 with no tailoring and no UK compliance awareness.
  5. It matches how you like to work. Deep-focus building against a defined system, not open-ended discovery. A catalogue turns your best hours into a reusable asset. Bespoke turns them into a one-off.
The condition this depends on

The catalogue is only profitable if the tailoring is genuinely bounded. If "a tailored first version" quietly becomes a bespoke build every time, £950 becomes loss-making at around eighteen hours. The single operational discipline that makes or breaks this business is your willingness to say "that is outside this route — here is what it would cost, or here is the direction that already does it." Write that sentence down now, before you need it.

Platform: one stack, bespoke, with a small editing layer

Recommendation: bespoke coded on your existing Jekyll/Netlify stack, plus a git-backed editing layer — Decap, Sveltia or CloudCannon — exposing only the fields that genuinely change: fees, availability, a short practice note, blog posts, and the contact block. Not the layout. Not the type. Not the colours.

This resolves the only real objection to bespoke, and it does it in a way Squarespace cannot match: the client gets to change what matters without being able to break what they paid you for. It also makes Website Care an honest convenience rather than a hostage arrangement, which matters enormously to a brand built on ethics.

Two commercial tiers on one technical platform. Never two technical platforms. If a buyer's real constraint is £250 rather than £950, they are not your customer, and the right answer is to say so warmly and point them at Your Therapy Website. Turning away the wrong client is a positioning act.

Shopify is not relevant. Therapists do not sell products; a handful sell courses or workbooks, and Stripe Payment Links or a single Gumroad page handles that without introducing an ecommerce platform. If a client ever needs a real shop, that is a referral, not a service line.

The scarcity rule — the most important idea in this document

Policy

Each direction is issued to a limited number of practices, and never twice to the same specialism in the same city or region. Publish the count remaining. When a direction is exhausted, retire it and replace it.

You are right to worry about the word "template". But the word is only unfair if it is untrue, and it is only untrue if two buyers cannot end up with recognisably the same website. A scarcity rule makes it structurally true rather than rhetorically denied. It does three things at once:

  • It protects the client's actual commercial interest — no therapist wants to discover the practice two towns over has their website.
  • It justifies the price without any sales language. Limited issue is a reason; "bespoke feel" is a claim.
  • It gives you a legitimate reason to retire and refresh directions, which keeps the catalogue alive without endless additions.

It also creates a genuine deadline in a market that has none, which is the single hardest problem with selling to therapists: nothing forces the decision. Judgement

What to build now — ranked 90-day plan

Ranked by what removes the largest obstacle to a first sale. Anything not on this list is in §8.

Days 1–14 · Make it possible to buy

Five things block a sale today. None of them is a design task.

  1. Set the price and collapse to two tiers. One hour of decision, half a day of edits across index.html, service.html, _pages/terms.html and _data/purchasing.yml, plus the QA price-consistency check. Nothing else can be sold until this is settled.
  2. Publish live demo subdomains for the six priority directions. (OPEN_DECISIONS #6.) The collection is your entire sales asset and seven of eight entries currently link to an enquiry form rather than a website. A buyer who cannot walk through the thing cannot choose it. Use the concept-safety rules you have already written — notice banner, noindex, no real contact details, inert forms.
  3. Settle ownership and licensing. (OPEN_DECISIONS #2.) My recommendation, in §7. This blocks going live and it is a fifteen-minute decision once you accept that generosity here costs you almost nothing.
  4. Fill the legal identity placeholders. (OPEN_DECISIONS #7.) Sole trader or limited, and a service address. Four contractual pages, one pass.
  5. Replace the mailto enquiry flow with real form handling. (OPEN_DECISIONS #9.) You correctly identified this as friction. It is worse than friction — a therapist reading your site on a phone will not compose and send an email to complete an enquiry. See §7 for how to do it without mishandling health data.

Days 1–90 · Twenty to thirty minutes a day, every day

The outreach runs in parallel with everything else and is never postponed for build work.

  1. Twenty named people. Cohort, placement colleagues, supervisor, tutors, local BACP network. One honest personal message each — not a pitch, a note saying what you have built and asking whether they know anyone setting up in practice.
  2. Three founding clients at a reduced fee, on the explicit condition of a named credit and a short written note about working with you. Say plainly that the price is founding because they are first. This is not discounting; it is buying evidence.
  3. Five supervisors, two course providers, two therapy-room rental businesses. Supervisors see newly qualified therapists constantly and are asked "how do I set up?" several times a year. One supervisor who mentions you to six supervisees is worth more than any social channel, and costs one email.

Days 15–45 · Make six directions unarguable

Not twelve. Six, finished to a standard where you would be content for a designer you respect to open the dev tools.

  1. The responsive and visual-QA foundation, applied identically across all six. This is the right priority and you have already named it. It is also the thing that makes six directions cost less than six times one direction.
  2. WCAG 2.2 AA across the six, tested not asserted — contrast, focus states, keyboard paths, form labels, headings, motion.
  3. Core Web Vitals within Google's thresholds (LCP ≤ 2.5s, INP ≤ 200ms, CLS ≤ 0.1) on a mid-range Android over 4G, not on your desktop.
  4. The content patterns that actually rank baked into every direction: a fees page, a "what happens in the first session" page, and a common questions page. These are the pages that win a therapist's local search terms. See §7 on E-E-A-T.
  5. The compliance layer. Intake captures professional body, membership number and exact permitted designation. Testimonials module off by default. A one-page "what you can and cannot say" sheet for clients. Details in §7 — this is a real differentiator and nobody else in the UK market appears to do it.
  6. Approve the questionnaire. (OPEN_DECISIONS #1.) The 15/7 draft is built. Approve it or replace it and set questionnaire_approved.

Days 46–90 · Deliver, measure, correct

The first builds are research, not just revenue.

  1. Ship the first paying build and time every stage. Intake, first version, each revision round, launch, handover. Your price is a hypothesis until you have three real timesheets.
  2. Publish the first real credited work the day it launches. One real named client outranks eight fictional concepts.
  3. Launch Website Care as an invoice, not a subscription. Recurring Stripe checkout is a materially larger piece of work — webhooks, dunning, cancellation, its own terms. Invoice annually until you have fifteen subscribers.
  4. Review at 90 days against three numbers only: enquiries received, proportion converted, and actual hours per build. Adjust price, not effort.
05

The first twelve directions

Answering the question you asked

Twelve is too many before you have sold anything. Build six to an unarguable standard; cap the visible collection at eight. Beyond eight, a browsing buyer stops choosing and starts deferring — and every additional direction multiplies your QA, accessibility and maintenance surface forever. Directions 9–12 below are a demand-led backlog, not a build list. Add one per two websites sold, and only when a buyer has asked for something the collection cannot do.

Eight exist. Four are proposed. Each entry states what makes it structurally rather than decoratively different — because if two directions differ only in palette, one of them is not a direction.

Rowan Hill

Built · Priority 1
For
The solo integrative or person-centred therapist working from home or a rented room. The largest single segment in the market.
Tone
Unhurried, sheltering, quietly warm. Room to arrive.
Structure
Short pages, long vertical rhythm, a single unforced enquiry route repeated calmly rather than urgently.
Visual
Generous whitespace, soft contrast, humanist serif headings at modest sizes; no hard edges, no strong grid.
Imagery
Landscape and natural light, never people in distress. Room interiors shot as spaces, not as staging.
Different because
It is the only direction that deliberately reduces information density. Everything else on this list adds structure; this one removes it.

Strongest commercially. Broadest audience, lowest objection rate, and the one most likely to be chosen by a first-time buyer.

Helen Calder

The established integrative practitioner, fifteen-plus years in, full or nearly full caseload, replacing a site built in 2014.

Assured, mature, unshowy. Someone with nothing to prove.

A substantial About that leads rather than follows; depth on approach; fees stated without apology.

One thing to fix in the current collection

Alexander Watson Counselling should not be a purchasable direction. It is your own practice's live website. Selling its design to another therapist means a buyer's site resembles yours — while you are also a practising counsellor in the same country. Keep it in the collection as a demonstration, clearly marked "the studio's own practice — not available", and let it do the credibility work it is genuinely good at. That also frees a slot in the visible eight.

Risks, blind spots and decisions you must make

Compliance — the biggest genuine gap, and a real opportunity

You are putting words on regulated professionals' websites. Nobody else in the UK market appears to handle this properly, and it is the most defensible differentiator available to you.

A live precedent, from 2025

Stockport Counselling Services was ruled against by the ASA over website copy stating the therapist was "accredited by" BACP when she was registered, not accredited. The advertiser said she had intended to remove the claim but "could not access the website to do so." The complaint was brought by BACP itself. Ruling published 12 March 2025.

Two lessons for you. The first is the copy risk. The second is quieter and more damaging: she could not edit her own site. That is exactly the failure mode a bespoke build creates — and exactly what your editing layer prevents.

  • Designation wording is exact. BACP permits only specific forms: "Registered Member MBACP", "Registered Member MBACP (Accred)", "Registered Member MBACP (Snr Accred)". On lapse, a member "may not refer to yourself using any designatory letters or titles" — immediately. The logo may be displayed but "only use the logos in their original format – don't change things like the colour, typeface, orientation or aspect ratio", which constrains your palette work. Verified · BACP
  • UKCP members may not use client testimonials at all. Code of Ethics 2019, clause 13: "Not make any claims which you cannot demonstrate to be true or include testimonials from clients in any advertising." Unambiguous. Verified · UKCP
  • ASA rules bite on everyone, member or not. CAP rule 12.2: claims to "diagnose, treat, help with or cure a medical condition" require appropriate qualification and evidence. "Helps with anxiety" is defensible; "treats anxiety" is not. Testimonials "alone do not constitute substantiation" — so a testimonial saying "she cured my panic attacks" is a rule 12.2 problem even where the body permits testimonials. Verified · ASA

What to build: intake captures professional body, membership number and category, and generates the exact permitted designation string. The testimonials module ships off and is only enabled after the body is confirmed. A one-page "what you can and cannot say on your website" sheet goes to every client. A clause in your terms has the client warrant that professional claims they supply are accurate. This costs you a day and is worth more than any design feature in the catalogue.

Enquiry forms and health data — the decision you are currently avoiding

Your forms assemble text in the browser and hand it back for the visitor to email themselves. That is honest and costs nothing, and you already flagged it as friction. It is worse than friction: a distressed person reading on a phone will not complete that flow. You are losing enquiries now, on your own site, and so would every client site built this way.

But the reason for the caution is real. The moment someone writes "I've been struggling with depression since March" into a message box, that is special-category health data under UK GDPR Article 9, which requires both an Article 6 lawful basis and a separate Article 9 condition. Verified · ICO

Recommendation. Use a UK or EU-hosted form handler, encrypted in transit, with no long-term storage of message bodies. Add microcopy at the message field asking people not to include clinical detail at this stage — that reduces the data collected and is also better practice clinically. Link the therapist's own privacy notice at the point of submission. Sign a short data processing agreement with each client where you configure the handler. On the ICO's own test, a studio that builds and hands over is typically neither controller nor processor — but if your tooling touches enquiry data on an ongoing basis, you become a processor with your own obligations. Decide that deliberately rather than discovering it. Verified · ICO

Ownership and licensing — my recommendation on OPEN_DECISIONS #2

Answer all three questions generously:

  • Perpetual, and independent of Website Care. A licence that dies when a subscription lapses is a hostage arrangement, and it is fatally at odds with a brand built on ethics and boundaries. It will also never be enforced.
  • They may move it anywhere and have anyone modify it — with support and warranty voided on modified code, stated plainly and without rancour.
  • One licence per practice website. A second site is a new licence, offered at a returning-client rate. That is a commercial opportunity, not a restriction.

The cost of this generosity is close to zero — almost no therapist will ever move a site — and the trust it buys in a profession that talks constantly to itself is considerable.

Accessibility — build it, don't sell fear with it

The Equality Act 2010 places an anticipatory reasonable-adjustments duty on service providers, and your clients are service providers. But WCAG is not itself UK law for private businesses, no UK case law was found establishing precedent against a private-sector site for inaccessibility, and the European Accessibility Act does not apply — it is not UK law and its scope list does not include a therapist's brochure site. Verified

So do not sell it as compliance risk; that would be the manipulative marketing you have said you will not do. Sell it as what it is: a meaningful proportion of the people arriving at a therapist's website are neurodivergent, visually impaired, or reading on a phone while distressed. Build to WCAG 2.2 AA, test it, say so plainly, and let it be a quiet quality signal against Wix and Squarespace builds. Judgement

SEO — what is actually true

Google states that for topics that "could significantly impact the health, financial stability, or safety of people" its systems give more weight to E-E-A-T, and that "of these aspects, trust is most important." Mental health is squarely in that category. Google also "strongly encourage[s] adding accurate authorship information." Verified · Google

Practically: a real About page with real credentials, real body links and a genuine bio is not a nicety, it is Google's stated signal for exactly this content type. Core Web Vitals are used in ranking but Google explicitly calls relevance primary — speed is a tiebreaker between two similar local competitors, which is often precisely the situation. Verified

Two things follow. Never sell ranking outcomes — sell foundations and content patterns, and say the difference out loud. And note the mismatch in your current content strategy: your guides library is excellent and it is written for therapists, who are your buyers. The pages that win your clients' local search terms are fees, first session, and common questions. Build those patterns into every direction. Judgement

Things you have not raised at all

  • You are a therapist marketing to therapists. You have said you are comfortable with your cohort, supervisor and tutors, and I agree there is no dual-relationship problem there. But write a short policy anyway: what happens if a studio client later wants to be a counselling client, or is a supervisee, or refers someone to you clinically. It is a five-line document and having it makes you the kind of practitioner therapists want to hire.
  • Your two businesses share your name. A prospective counselling client googling you finds a studio; a prospective studio client finds a counsellor. This is probably fine and arguably an asset — but decide it consciously and make sure each site handles the crossover gracefully.
  • You are a single point of failure for every site you build. Mitigate cheaply: the client's domain and hosting live in the client's own accounts, never yours; the handover pack is genuinely sufficient for another developer; static hosting means nothing breaks if you disappear for a month. Three decisions, all free, that make the offer materially safer to buy.
  • VAT is a pricing cliff, not a tax detail. Your clients are almost all unregistered sole traders who cannot reclaim VAT, so crossing the registration threshold makes you 20% more expensive overnight to every buyer. At Year 3 base you are not near it; at raised prices plus care you are approaching it. Check the current threshold and model the crossing before it happens, with an accountant. Judgement
  • Consumer cancellation rights on the online purchase. A sole-trader therapist buying personally may count as a consumer, which brings a 14-day cancellation right on distance-sold services unless they expressly request that work begins immediately and acknowledge losing the right. That express waiver is a specific mechanism, not a general clause. Add it to your solicitor's list.
  • Every portfolio piece is fictional. Eight invented practices is impressive craft and it has a hard ceiling — buyers pattern-match to "has not done this for real." Three named credited clients changes the business more than three new directions would.
  • Concentration risk. One country, one profession, one offer. If a funded competitor enters or the sector softens you have no second leg. Do not build one — but know that supervisors and trainers, celebrants, osteopaths and small charities are tonally adjacent, and that your system would transfer.

The five decisions only you can make

  1. Price. £950 and £2,450, or your own numbers — but two tiers, decided this week, and not £495.
  2. Licence terms. The three answers above, or your own. It blocks going live.
  3. Legal identity. Sole trader or limited, and the address you publish.
  4. Form handling. Real submission with a considered data posture, or keep the mailto flow and accept the enquiry loss. There is no third option.
  5. Founding clients. Whether you will offer three reduced-fee places in exchange for named credit. This is the fastest route out of the fictional-portfolio problem and it has a cost.
