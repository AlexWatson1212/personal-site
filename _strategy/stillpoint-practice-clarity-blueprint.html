Stillpoint Practice Clarity Blueprint

The practice reasoning behind Stillpoint Therapy, reconstructed from the finished concept: who Michael Deane appears to be, who he naturally speaks to, what he believes makes therapy useful, and why the website behaves and looks as it does.

01

Practice at a glance

Three lines on the homepage carry the whole positioning, and they have to be read together rather than separately.

Somewhere to stop for a while.

Most people who come here are not in crisis. They are managing. They are going to work, answering messages, doing the things that need doing.

They have simply reached a point where something needs saying out loud, and there is nowhere ordinary to say it.

Home

The first line offers a place and a duration, and nothing else — no outcome, no method, no promise about who you will be afterwards. The second removes the entry requirement most people assume therapy has: you do not have to be falling apart to qualify. The third supplies the actual reason for the practice's existence. It is not that these people cannot speak. It is that the settings available to them have run out of room.

Michael's own account of what he learned in the hospice says the same thing from the practitioner's side — "people mainly need somewhere to put things down" — and the bereavement page names the mechanism directly: a difficulty becomes unbearable when it becomes unspeakable, and "sealing it off costs energy you cannot spare."

The organising idea

The proposed wording — an ordinary, undramatised place to stop and speak about experiences that have become difficult to carry alone — is close, but it flattens a distinction the concept is careful about. Stillpoint does not undramatise the experience. It is unusually blunt about how bad things are: "People are kind for about a month. Then everyone goes back to their lives, and you are still in it." What it undramatises is therapy — "two people in chairs having a serious conversation, which is a fairly ordinary thing that has been made to sound strange."

That asymmetry is the strategy. The proposition, restated:

A regular, unremarkable hour in which something that has become unsayable everywhere else can be put down and said properly.

Proposed core proposition

The emotional function follows from it. Stillpoint does not lower the seriousness of what people bring; it lowers their sense of how much entitlement they need in order to bring it. The therapy page states this outright: "You are allowed to come here without a diagnosis, without a crisis, and without a clear reason." Inference For an audience whose main obstacle is the private thought this probably isn't bad enough, that permission is the single most useful thing the site can do.

Bereavement and life changes are the two doors, not the definition. Described as "counselling for bereavement and life changes in Lewes", the practice loses the part that makes it distinctive: it is organised around the absence of anywhere to speak, not around a list of presenting problems.

02

Professional identity

I am quiet in the room. I ask questions when they are useful and stay out of the way when they are not. I do not talk in the language of therapy, and I will not ask you to.

Home

Identity statement. An experienced counsellor who treats sustained attention as the whole of the skill, and who has removed from the work — and from the way he describes it — everything that would require a client to perform.

Distinctive qualities

Deflationary about his own craft. He describes the hospice as teaching him that the work takes "far less cleverness and far more patience than I had assumed"; calls the evidence for the therapeutic relationship "the least glamorous finding in the whole field"; and titles a section There is less mystery to it than people imagine. Expertise is real and eighteen years deep, but it is never staged.

Quiet as method, not temperament. The restraint is described as deliberate and reasoned: "I ask a lot fewer questions than people expect. When I do ask something, it is usually because I have not understood, not because I am steering you somewhere." Silence is claimed as competence — "If you go quiet, I will not fill it for you unless you want me to." This is not passivity; it is a decision about who is allowed to set the pace.

Direct, and correctable. The unusual half of the directness is the second half: "If I think you are avoiding something, I will say so, and you are free to tell me I am wrong. If I have got something badly wrong, I would rather know." He builds the client's right to overrule him into the description of the work, and repeats it as a practical instruction on the therapy page: if it isn't working, "say so."

Unfrightened by the material. Eleven years in hospice and bereavement services, and copy that goes where sympathy cards do not: relief and anger after a death, a grief with no social permission attached, "grieving someone you did not entirely like." The therapeutic claim rests on it — change happens when something is said "to someone who is not frightened of it."

Unhurried, as a stated resource. Time is what he says he has and other settings do not: talking about the person who died "for as long as it takes, which is usually longer than anyone has given you"; with people mid-transition, "first, we would slow it down."

Bounded in public. The site publishes its own limits without apology: no couples, families or children; no assessment or diagnosis; no reports for courts, employers or insurers; not an emergency service; second floor, no lift. Inference Publishing the boundaries is a trust device rather than a disclaimer — a practitioner who names what he will not do is more believable about what he will.

Tested and set aside: warm and empathetic are true of any competent therapist and are not claimed anywhere on the site. Passive is wrong — he interrupts avoidance. Austere is wrong too; the writing is dry rather than cold, and repeatedly funny in a way that only works if he likes people.

03

Philosophy of change

The concept states a mechanism rather than a promise, and states it in three places that agree with each other.

That is not a technique; it is what happens when something gets said out loud, repeatedly, to someone who is not frightened of it.

Therapy

What tends to change is not the size of the loss but how much of your life it takes to carry.

Loss & Bereavement

Defining belief

Difficulty becomes unbearable largely because it becomes unsayable. Said out loud, repeatedly, to someone unfrightened by it, it does not get smaller — it simply takes less of a life to carry.

Two corollaries run through the site. First, that much of this is not illness: "Grief is not an illness. Neither is realising at fifty-two that you no longer recognise the life you built. These are problems of meaning, and they respond to being thought about rather than treated." Second, that the relationship, not the model, is the active ingredient — which is why the integrative training is explained in one paragraph and then set aside.

What Michael believes

Sealing something off is what costs; speech is the work.

What Michael does

No exercises, no worksheets, nothing to do between sessions, no rating your mood out of ten. The hour is for talking.

What the client experiences

Nothing to prepare, revise or perform. The only task is the one they came to do.

What Michael believes

The relationship does most of the work, so it has to be examinable.

What Michael does

Invites contradiction, reviews the work out loud every couple of months, and treats "this isn't working" as useful information.

What the client experiences

Doubt can be spoken to his face without ending anything or becoming a failure.

What Michael believes

Most of this is meaning, not pathology.

What Michael does

Declines diagnosis, assessment, reports and health insurance — insurers "require a diagnosis, session limits and reports, none of which fit the way I work."

What the client experiences

Arrives as a person with a situation rather than a patient with a condition.

What Michael believes

Carrying capacity changes; the loss does not.

What Michael does

Promises no recovery, closure or stages — and explicitly refuses stage theory, because being told grief has stages "tends to make people feel they are doing it incorrectly."

What the client experiences

No schedule to fall behind, and no way to be grieving wrongly.

04

The people he naturally helps

The homepage describes them by what they are still doing, not by what is wrong with them — a portrait of competence with something underneath it.

Visible from outside
Full functioning. Work, messages, the school run, the estate paperwork. Nobody is worried about them.Adults, individually; a lot of people in their forties, fifties and sixties. More than half of them men.
Happening inside
Something has ended, accumulated, arrived late, or never got said. In the concept's own registers: grief that "arrived late"; a marriage "that has become silent"; the sense that you are "living a life that belongs to somebody else."
Why ordinary support runs out
Four distinct reasons appear in the copy: kindness has a half-life ("kind for about a month"); the material is repetitive and they will not raise it a fifth time; it is socially inadmissible (relief at a death, an ex-partner's death, drinking, saying so "would sound ungrateful"); or the people they would tell are the subject.
Why therapy feels unavailable
An expectation of emotional performance — "a reasonable suspicion that therapy might involve being asked to perform an emotion on demand" — plus jargon, the television version, being analysed, and not knowing what you are supposed to say. Deliberation is long: "somewhere between six months and nine years."
What they actually want
Modest and specific. To say a thing properly, once, to someone who will not flinch. To understand what happened. To stop being the only one holding it. Not transformation — the site never offers any.

The three doors, and why the third one is unusual

The homepage offers exactly three routes: After a death, When things change, If therapy is unfamiliar. The first two are circumstances. The third is not a problem at all — it is an objection, given equal weight, a full page and a permanent slot in the navigation. Inference For an audience that has deliberated for years, unfamiliarity is the presenting problem, and burying it in an FAQ would treat the largest barrier as a footnote.

The For Men page then handles the demographic reality without the usual rhetoric. It opens by refusing the premise — "This page is not about men being bad at feelings", and "men talk perfectly well once it is clear what the thing is for" — and reframes the difficulty as practice and setting rather than capacity. It then answers logistics: silence, having nothing to say, whether he will tell your GP, whether one session is allowed. Inference Michael is credible to this reader precisely because he has already said the awkward parts out loud, so they are not required to ask.

05

Messaging and voice

Primary message

You do not have to become a different kind of person, or explain yourself first, to use this.

Supporting themes

  1. Nothing is required in advance. No diagnosis, no crisis, no clear reason, no vocabulary. "Two lines is enough."
  2. The practical facts are all here, in full. Fee, address, days, cancellation terms, what is written down, who could ever see it — published rather than available on request.
  3. Difficulty is described accurately, not softened. The site names relief, anger, guilt, drinking, an unmourned father, a death nobody counted.

Tone of voice

Plain · specific · deflationary · unhurried · dry · unpersuading. It has one quality most therapy writing lacks entirely: exact, unshowy humour — "the thing you built your identity on can be taken away by email"; "your body has started making decisions without consulting you." The jokes are never at the reader's expense, and they are the clearest evidence that the restraint is not coldness.

Why the copy stops early

Its most characteristic move is to make an observation and then decline to interpret it. "People are kind for about a month. Then everyone goes back to their lives, and you are still in it." No consolation follows. "Nothing much happens here, which is the point." No elaboration follows.

This does two things at once. It leaves the reader to supply their own case, so the writing fits a bereaved husband and a redundant fifty-five-year-old without addressing either by name. And it demonstrates the room: a thing can be said here without anyone rushing in to soften or solve it. Inference The space after the sentence is doing the same work Michael says he does in person — it is the page not filling the silence.

Language to embrace

  • Ordinary nouns, ordinary verbs, no register above the reader's
  • Real numbers: 55 minutes, £70, £45, 20 minutes, 48 hours, two working days
  • Observations the reader recognises before they agree with
  • Admissions of limit — what he will not do, and what he cannot
  • The reader's own words for things, including their scepticism

Language to avoid

  • The concept's own blacklist: "No inner child, no journey, no holding space."
  • Safe space, healing, transformation, empowerment, authentic self
  • Stages, closure, or any implied timetable for grief
  • Reassurance offered before it has been earned
  • Anything that reads as an argument for having therapy
06

Intended client experience

Feel
Unhurried. Unobserved. Not required to perform, or to have an emotion ready. Taken seriously without being made into a patient. Allowed to stop reading, stop scrolling, or stop after one session. Allowed to say it badly.Not "calm" — calm is the by-product. The operative feeling is nothing is being asked of me yet.
Understand
What Michael is like to sit with; what an hour actually contains; what it costs and where it happens; and whether this kind of work applies to them at all.The site answers "what will this be like?" before "what is therapy?" — the order the anxious reader asks in.
Trust
Built by disclosure rather than assertion. Eighteen years and eleven in hospice work are stated once, flatly, in a ledger. Registration, supervision, insurance and the BACP framework are given as checkable facts, not badges. Prices are published in a table. The exceptions to confidentiality are listed in full, including the ones that are not flattering.The strongest device is on the Fees page: NHS talking therapies, Cruse and hospice services are recommended by name, in the one place where a sale is at stake. Nothing else on the site proves as much.
Do next
Arrange a first conversation — never "start therapy". An email or voicemail, replied to within two working days, then twenty free minutes whose stated purpose is "to work out whether to meet."For someone who has thought about this for six months to nine years, the obstacle is not information but irreversibility. So the next step is made reversible, and the site blesses the exit: "Plenty of people stop there, which is a perfectly good outcome."
07

Strategic translation into the website

Each decision below is present in the built concept. The test applied to every row is the same: why does this make sense for Michael, and for the person he naturally works with?

Strategic insightWebsite consequence
Michael's method is subtraction: he removes what would make a client perform. The interface subtracts too. One typeface across the whole site, no icons, no cards, no testimonials, no badges, no pop-ups. Restraint is not a taste preference here — it is the same decision as not filling the silence.
The reader is deliberating, not shopping, and has usually been deliberating for years. Long uninterrupted intervals between sections, and whole bands carrying a single sentence. The page declines the commercial instinct to fill every viewport with persuasion, and lets the reader stop mid-page without being chased."Nothing much happens here, which is the point."
He is credentialled but refuses to stage expertise. Type is set small and serif — body at 17px, and a display size that tops out barely above it. The largest words on the site are only slightly larger than the smallest. Nothing is shouted, so nothing has to be believed on the strength of its size.
He will not tell anyone how their grief or their divorce is supposed to feel. Colour is almost entirely absent: ink, fog, paper, stone. The single accent — a low rust — appears only on links and hover states. Colour is reserved for things you can do, never for mood. The site issues no emotional instruction.
These clients are wary of being depicted, and of aspirational imagery. Landscape and surface only: an estuary at flat light, reeds dissolving into mist, still water, a rendered wall in raking light. No people, no consulting-room staging, no soft-focus hands. The images give atmosphere without telling anyone what they should be feeling in it.
The practice is about a moment before change, not a resolution. The homepage caption does the positioning without explaining itself."The Ouse below Lewes, in the hour before the weather changes."Place, waiting and imminent change in eleven words — factual enough to read as a caption, exact enough to be the thesis.
The first question is the visitor's, not the therapist's. There is no portrait of Michael anywhere on the site. He is present as a first-person voice and as one wall of the building he works in. The opening screens stay with the reader's own situation instead of asking them to assess a stranger's face.
Atmosphere alone would leave the reader with nobody to meet. The only charcoal field on the homepage is Michael's introduction. It is the strongest tonal shift on the page and it lands on the one human moment — a deliberate weight after a spacious opening. Elsewhere the dark field is reserved for the hardest direct address: the questions people ask on the bereavement and For Men pages, the urgent-help notice on Contact. Three pages carry no dark section at all, so it never becomes a rhythm.
The room is real and modest; the practice is not a lifestyle. The consulting room is represented by a texture — plaster in raking light — captioned with a fact about the building's age. It establishes somewhere real without staging an aspirational interior, which would be a claim about status rather than about work.
A first decision is only useful if it is small. Three pathways, not twenty service cards. Two circumstances and one objection are enough to route almost every visitor, and a short list can be read whole. Fragmenting into specialisms would imply that arriving with the wrong label disqualifies you — the opposite of the site's actual message.
Emotional restraint has to be paid for with practical certainty. The sparse site becomes unusually concrete about logistics: 55 minutes, weekly, same time; Fisher Street, Tuesday to Thursday; online Monday and Friday; £70, with reduced places at £45 and no means test; 48 hours' notice; second floor, no lift. The atmosphere is allowed to be quiet because none of the facts are.
Being made to ask the price is a small humiliation. Fees are a plain table on a page in the main navigation, opening with the reason for its own existence. Reduced-fee places are described in a way that costs the applicant nothing: "There is no form and no means test. You tell me it would help, I take you at your word, and we do not discuss it again."
Contact is the point of maximum reluctance. The Contact page is built entirely around one line — Two lines is enough — with a short form, an optional phone field, a plain consent checkbox, and a stated reply time. What happens next is described before the form is submitted, so nobody sends a message into silence.

Two further implementation details support the reading rather than decorate it: the layout is built on a fixed frame with a marginal label rail and a roughly 33rem reading measure, so no line stretches on a wide screen; and asymmetric sections are offset at 38.2% — a golden section — which is why the negative space reads as composed rather than left over.

08

Practice clarity snapshot

Professional identity
An experienced counsellor — eighteen years, eleven of them in hospice and bereavement work — who treats sustained attention as the whole of the skill. Quiet by method rather than temperament, direct enough to be told he is wrong, and unwilling to use the language of therapy at a client.
Ideal client
An adult who is still functioning perfectly well and is carrying something that has run out of anywhere to be said — a death, an ending, a change they did not choose — and who is wary of therapy's manner rather than its usefulness.
Defining belief
Difficulty becomes unbearable largely because it becomes unsayable; said out loud, repeatedly, to someone unfrightened by it, it takes less of a life to carry.
Core message
Somewhere to stop for a while — no crisis, no vocabulary and no explanation required in advance.
Voice
Plain · specific · deflationary · unhurried · dry · unpersuading
Desired feeling
Unhurried · unobserved · taken seriously · allowed to stop
Visual direction
An editorial object rather than a website: one serif across everything, small and evenly scaled; ink, fog, paper and stone with a single low rust reserved for things you can click; a fixed frame with marginal labels and a narrow reading measure; landscape and surface photography with no people in it, varied in shape so no two pages share a skeleton; charcoal fields spent only on the most direct address; motion slow enough to be noticed only in its absence.
Website priority
Let someone decide whether they could sit opposite this particular person — and then make the first step small enough to take today.
Principle that should survive every redesign
The site must never ask the visitor to be more certain, more articulate or more distressed than they actually are.

The chain the concept has to hold: a quiet, deflationary practitioner → clients who are competent, private and out of places to speak → who need permission and practical certainty rather than persuasion → a belief that speech, not technique, is what changes the weight of things → writing that observes and then stops → a site that offers a small reversible step and publishes everything else → a restrained editorial surface that issues no emotional instruction. Read backwards, every visible decision has somewhere to land.
