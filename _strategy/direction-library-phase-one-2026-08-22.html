How this answers the audit

Two challenge the calm-beige convention outright. Broadsheet is near-white on near-black with a single vermilion and no botanical imagery at all. Sounding is the only dark direction in the library.

Only two of six are serif-led, against seven of seven now. Two are sans-only, one is grotesque-only, one is single-family.

Absorptions. D4 supersedes Different Minds and inherits its components. D5 absorbs Common Ground. D6 inherits Face to Face's structural work but is re-shaped for a private practice. Harbour, North & Vale and Stillpoint are out of scope for these six and stay as portfolio evidence.

Greyscale test — a soft five-step ramp. Reads as quiet and low-contrast even without hue, which is the intent here.

Therapy in Shrewsbury, at an unhurried pace

The same family sets the body text. One typeface for the whole site, sized and spaced rather than contrasted — the distinguishing typographic decision of this direction.

Petrona 400/500 · fallback: Georgia, "Iowan Old Style", serif · base 18px / 1.7

Best suited to
Solo integrative, person-centred or humanistic counsellor, three to fifteen years qualified, working from a home room or a rented room one or two days a week. Client group: adults 28–55, usually first time in therapy, anxious about the enquiry itself. Anxiety, low mood, life transitions, self-worth. Practice stage: established enough to have a waiting list occasionally, not building a brand. Tone: unhurried, sheltering, adult.
Journey
Arrive, read to the end, enquire. One visit. A quiet anchored index follows the scroll but there is nowhere else to go — the sub-pages exist for people who want more, not because the homepage withheld anything.
Layout
Single 60ch column throughout. No cards, no grids, no tiles, no icon rows. Sections are separated by vertical space and one full-measure hairline. Two images only: one at the fold, one at the close.
Typography
Petrona at 400 and 500 for everything, including body. Headings are large and light; body is 18px at 1.7. The hierarchy is size and space, never a second family.
Imagery
Interior light only. The room, the chair, the window, the door — shot at seated eye height, natural light, no people. Explicitly not landscape, not plants, not hands, not a mug.
Voice
Second person, short declarative sentences, no metaphor at all. "You can email me and I will reply within two working days." Never "a space to explore", never "a journey".
Motif
A hairline rule at the full text measure. It is the only ornament in the direction; nothing else is decorative.
Different because
It is the only direction that removes structure. Every other one on this list adds a device — a dateline, a summary box, a paired panel, a status table. This one has a single column and a rule, and its quality is entirely in spacing, measure and restraint. It is also the only single-typeface direction.
Worked example, brand system and website system

Fictional worked example

Wenlock Counselling — Shrewsbury, Shropshire. Practitioner: Ruth Sandiford. Positioning line: "Counselling in Shrewsbury, at an unhurried pace." Integrative counsellor, adults, one-to-one, in person and online. Fee stated as £55, with two lower-cost places noted as sometimes available.

Concept disclosure, on every page: "This is a design concept by Alexander Watson Studio. Wenlock Counselling is not a real practice and no service is offered here." Professional status appears only as a placeholder — [Registration body] · [Membership category] — never a body name, number, qualification, insurer or claim. No testimonials. No statement that anything is treated or cured.

Wordmark concept

Set, not drawn: the practice name in Petrona 400 at a generous tracking of 0.02em, with the place name beneath in the same face at 55% size and 0.14em. Implemented as inline SVG with the text converted to outlines at build time so the mark is stable and does not reflow. No symbol, no monogram, no leaf. A single hairline sits above the name at the same weight as the section rule, tying the mark to the page's only ornament.

Spacing, border, button, form

  • Spacing scale: 8 / 16 / 24 / 40 / 64 / 96 / 144. Section rhythm is 96 on mobile, 144 on desktop — the most generous in the library, and the thing that makes the direction work.
  • Borders: 1px only, and only --hairline horizontally. No boxes, no radii above 0, no shadows anywhere.
  • Buttons: one style — a filled moss rectangle, 4px radius, 52px tall, 24px horizontal padding, white label at 15px/600. One primary per section, maximum. Secondary actions are underlined text links, never outlined buttons.
  • Forms: 1px --rule-strong border, 4px radius, 52px tall, 16px text so iOS does not zoom, label always visible above the field, never a placeholder as a label. Focus is a 2px moss outline at 2px offset.

Image art direction and prompts

Photographed, not generated, wherever the client can manage it — the research on client choice weights the real photograph heavily. Where a placeholder is needed:

Interior of a small counselling room in an English house, photographed at seated eye height. Late afternoon daylight from a sash window at frame left. One upholstered armchair in muted olive, a low side table, a plain wall. No people, no plants in flower, no candles, no cushions arranged. Natural colour, slightly cool shadows, 35mm, f/4, no vignette.

A Georgian terraced doorway on a quiet Shropshire street, closed, painted a soft dark green, brass numeral. Overcast even light. Shot straight on from six feet. No people, no signage, no car. Documentary, not architectural-magazine.

Voice guidance

Write as though speaking to one person who has already decided to look. Sentences under 20 words. Say what happens, in order. Answer the practical question before the emotional one. Never open with a question. Never describe the therapy as a space, a journey, a container or a safe place.

Avoid list

Cards. Icon rows. Testimonials. Nature photography. Plants. Stones. Sunsets. Open hands. Mugs. Script or handwriting faces. Any second typeface. Colour outside the token set. Drop shadows. Parallax. Fade-in-on-scroll. The words journey, space, safe space, unpack, hold, container, authentic self, wellness, holistic. Any claim to treat or cure.

Accessibility notes

Base 18px. Line length capped at 68 characters at every breakpoint. Target size 52px on all primary controls, above the 44px WCAG 2.2 floor. Contrast measured above. Because the direction is one long page, the anchored index must be a real <nav> with a skip link ahead of it, and each anchored section needs a focusable heading. No motion beyond a 150ms colour transition, disabled under prefers-reduced-motion.

Fixed versus customisable

Direction-fixed: single column, single typeface, no cards, section rhythm, the hairline motif, page count, homepage order, button and form rules.
Client-customisable: practice and place name, all words, the two photographs, the accent within a supplied set of four tested alternatives to moss, fee figures, contact route, and one optional extra page from a defined menu.

Website system

Sitemap: Home · About · Fees and practicalities · Contact · Privacy · Accessibility · 404. Five visible pages, two in the footer.

Homepage order: (1) Name and one-sentence positioning, no image above it. (2) Who I work with, plainly listed — the specificity the market evidence says is usually buried. (3) What happens when you get in touch, in three steps with real timings. (4) About, short, with the portrait. (5) Fee, session length, in person and online, location. (6) Enquiry. (7) Urgent-support boundary, always last, never styled as an alarm.

Components: anchored index, hairline section rule, step list, fee block, enquiry form, urgent-support panel, registration placeholder, concept notice.

Calls to action: one, repeated verbatim three times — "Send an enquiry". No second verb anywhere.

SEO: one town, one modality, one client group. Title pattern Counselling in [town] — [Name]. A real About page with the practitioner as a named author, per Google's stated E-E-A-T weighting for health topics. LocalBusiness structured data only if the client publishes a real address; never MedicalBusiness, never a named-person health schema.

Mobile priorities: the fee and the enquiry route must both be reachable within two thumb-scrolls. The portrait loads at 2:3, not 16:9. The anchored index collapses to a single sticky "Contact" affordance rather than a menu.

Trust and factual checklist: fee stated · session length stated · in-person and online stated · town stated · response time stated · registration placeholder present and unfilled · urgent-support boundary present · privacy notice covering enquiry data · no testimonials · no treatment claims · concept disclosure on every page.

Content required from the client: roughly 700 words across five sections, one professional portrait, two room or place photographs, fee and session facts, and their exact registration wording as issued by their body.

Commercial fit

Straightforward Website. It is the default choice and will be the most-picked, so it must be deliverable with almost no decisions. The content burden on the client is the lowest of the six, the page count is the lowest, and there is no editorial work to do beyond shaping what they send. It is not a Bespoke candidate — its whole value is that it is finished.

Greyscale test — the widest tonal spread in the library. Near-white against near-black is legible as a distinct design with the hue removed entirely.

On being tired in a way sleep doesn't fix

No serif appears anywhere in this direction. Hierarchy comes from weight, size and tracking within one grotesque, which is what gives it its contemporary register.

Schibsted Grotesk 400/600 · fallback: Helvetica Neue, Arial, sans-serif · headings tracked −0.03em

Best suited to
A therapist who writes and is read — a Substack, a column, a book in progress, conference papers. Often works with people in media, academia, the arts and the third sector; often specialises in meaning, identity, work, burnout, or the social dimension of distress. Client group: 25–45, culturally engaged, allergic to wellness language, likely to read three pieces before enquiring. Practice stage: established, with a public voice already. Tone: literate, direct, unsentimental, alive.
Journey
Multi-visit by design. Arrive from a shared link, read, leave, return, then enquire. The only direction where the enquiry is not expected on the first visit, and the only one whose homepage changes when the client publishes.
Layout
A masthead with a real dateline and a thick top rule. Asymmetric two-column below: the current piece takes two-thirds, a standing column of practice facts takes one-third and stays put. Archive is a dated list, not a card grid.
Typography
One grotesque, no serif at all. Headlines at 600 with tight negative tracking; body at 400/1.62. Standfirsts at 1.25rem in muted. The type is the design.
Imagery
No nature, no interiors, no stock. Black-and-white portraiture, editorial illustration, or nothing — and "nothing" is a legitimate finished state here. Where an image runs, it runs full-bleed and uncaptioned.
Voice
Essayistic first person. Opinions actually held. No hedging, no "it can be helpful to". Titles are statements, not questions.
Motif
Press furniture: the dateline, the 4px top rule, the vertical column rule, the small-caps section kicker. Nothing organic anywhere.
Different because
It is the only direction where the homepage is editorial rather than promotional, and the only one that gets better the longer the client uses it. It is also the deliberate opposite of the category norm — the audit found seven of seven warm and serif-led; this is cool, high-contrast and sans-only.

Concept disclosure, on every page: "A design concept by Alexander Watson Studio. Marchmont Practice is not a real practice and no service is offered here." Image art direction and prompts

Black-and-white environmental portrait of a woman in her forties at a desk by a tall Georgian window, Edinburgh tenement interior, mid-morning. She is looking away from camera, mid-thought, not smiling. Grain visible, deep blacks, 50mm, f/2. Documentary register, not corporate headshot. No props, no bookshelf staging.

Black-and-white, high contrast: rain on a sash window with an out-of-focus stone tenement beyond. No people. Abstract enough to run full-bleed behind a headline without competing with it.

Illustration alternative: single-weight line drawing in ink on paper, one colour, no gradients — commissioned, never generated, if the client can afford it.

Voice guidance

Write essays, not blog posts. Begin in the middle. Use the first person and mean it. Cite what you have actually read. Do not end with a call to action — the standing column already carries it. The practice pages should read as though written by the same person as the essays, which means the About page can be interesting rather than reassuring.

Avoid list

Any serif. Warm neutrals. Beige, cream, sage, terracotta. Nature photography of any kind. Stock imagery. Rounded corners. Soft shadows. Pastel anything. Inspirational quotations. Pull-quotes in script. The words curated, mindful, holistic, wellness, thought leader, deep dive. Newsletter pop-ups. Reading-time estimates. Social share counts.

Accessibility notes

High contrast is an asset here but the tight tracking is a risk: never track body text, only headlines above 28px. Base 17px minimum. The asymmetric grid must reflow to a single column below 900px with the standing column moving below the current piece, not above it. Archive dates need <time datetime>. The masthead rule is decorative and must be aria-hidden. Essays need real heading hierarchy — this is the only direction where long-form structure carries screen-reader navigation.

Fixed versus customisable

Direction-fixed: the grotesque-only rule, radius 0, the masthead and dateline, the asymmetric grid, the archive-as-list, the absence of testimonials, vermilion reserved from buttons.
Client-customisable: the accent hue within four tested alternatives to vermilion, all words, portrait, essay taxonomy, whether the standing column shows fee or availability first.

Website system

Sitemap: Home (front page) · Writing (archive) · Writing/[piece] · Therapy · About · Contact · Privacy · Accessibility · 404.

Homepage order: (1) Masthead with dateline. (2) The current piece — headline, standfirst, first three paragraphs, continue link. (3) Standing column: who I work with, fee, where, next step. (4) Three previous pieces as a dated list. (5) About, two paragraphs. (6) Enquiry. (7) Urgent-support boundary.

Components: masthead, dateline, standfirst, standing column, archive list, tag chip, essay layout, enquiry form, urgent-support panel, registration placeholder, concept notice.

Calls to action: two, deliberately unequal — "Read more writing" as the primary in-page action, "Enquire about therapy" standing quietly in the fixed column. The direction accepts a slower conversion in exchange for a better-qualified one.

SEO: the strongest organic candidate of the six, because it publishes. Essays carry author bylines linking to a substantive About page, which is exactly what Google says it weights for health topics. Article structured data on essays; no health or medical schema anywhere.

Mobile priorities: the standing column must not become a wall of facts between the headline and the essay — it moves below the fold. Reading measure caps at 66 characters. Tap targets in the archive list are full-row, not just the title.

Trust and factual checklist: fee stated in the standing column · location and online stated · response time stated · registration placeholder present · urgent-support boundary present · essays clearly dated · author identified · privacy notice covering enquiry data · no testimonials component in the build at all · no treatment claims · concept disclosure on every page.

Content required from the client: three finished essays of 800–1,500 words before launch, and a realistic commitment to one more per quarter. One black-and-white portrait. This is the highest content burden in the library and the direction fails without it.

Sales rule for this direction

Do not sell Broadsheet to a therapist who does not already write. An empty archive makes the homepage look abandoned within three months, which is worse than the site they had. Ask to see three existing pieces before accepting the brief.

Commercial fit

Guided Website, or Practice Clarity + Bespoke. Not Straightforward. The direction depends on editorial judgement — what to publish, how to title it, how the essays and the practice pages speak in one voice — and that is exactly the work the Guided tier exists to sell. Where the therapist has a public profile worth positioning around, it justifies the Bespoke tier on its own.

What open-ended therapy asks of you

Old-style figures, true italics used for emphasis rather than bold, and leading at 1.75 — the most generous in the library, because reading light text on a dark ground is harder and the design has to pay for it.

EB Garamond 400 + italic · fallback: Garamond, Georgia, serif · base 19px / 1.75 · measure 56ch

Best suited to
Psychodynamic, Jungian, existential or relational psychotherapist, typically UKCP or BPC registered, offering weekly or twice-weekly open-ended work and often taking supervisees. Client group: 30–60, frequently has had therapy before and wants something longer, willing to commit time and money. Practice stage: senior; may be a training therapist. Tone: contained, serious, quiet, unhurried to the point of stillness.
Journey
Deliberately slow and deliberately filtered. There is no booking button. The next step is a written enquiry followed by a paid initial consultation, and the site says so plainly. It converts fewer visitors on purpose, and better ones.
Layout
Dark ground, 56ch measure, very large top and bottom margins. Sections divided by space alone — no rules, no boxes, no alternating grounds. The page is mostly empty and that is the point.
Typography
EB Garamond at 19px, 1.75 leading, old-style figures. Italic does the work bold does elsewhere. Headings are barely larger than body — hierarchy is space, not scale.
Imagery
Almost none. One abstract long-exposure or texture image, or none at all. No portrait above the fold — the practitioner appears once, low on the About page.
Voice
Third person when describing the work, first person sparingly and only where it is genuinely personal. Complete paragraphs, not fragments. The one direction where a long sentence is correct.
Motif
A single thin vertical rule at the left edge of the reading column, running the full height of the text — a manuscript rule. It is the only line on the page.
Different because
Two structural inversions, not one. It is the only dark direction; and it is the only one not organised by presenting problem. There is no "I can help with anxiety, depression, trauma" grid. The navigation is the shape of the work: what open-ended therapy is, what frequency means, the first three sessions, and how endings are handled.

Image art direction and prompts

The exterior of a modest town-centre building entrance in a northern English city, painted door, visible street number, ordinary daylight, a passer-by out of focus. Shot straight on from across the pavement. Documentary, unstyled, honest about the building being unglamorous.

A small plain waiting area: three chairs against a wall, a low table with a box of tissues and a jug of water, a noticeboard with leaflets. No people. Ordinary overhead lighting, not corrected to look warm. The photograph should reassure by being real rather than by being beautiful.

Voice guidance

Lead with the number. Answer the money question in the first sentence. Use short paragraphs and plain words at roughly a reading age of nine — not because readers are unsophisticated but because they may be distressed, rushed, or reading on a phone with poor signal. Never apologise for the fee being low; never imply the service is lesser for it.

Avoid list

Aspirational hero headlines. Full-bleed imagery above the fold. Nature. Stock photography of any kind. The words affordable, competitive, budget, reduced-rate, deserving, worthy. Any implication of means-testing beyond what is genuinely operated. Charity framing for a private practice. Fake urgency about places remaining. Any waiting-time figure that is not actually maintained.

Accessibility notes

The status panel is a real <table> or a description list with proper labels, never a visual grid of divs, so it can be read in order by assistive technology. Tabular figures throughout. On mobile it becomes stacked label-value rows, never a horizontally scrolling table. Notices carry a word as well as a colour. The "last checked" date is real and machine-readable, and if it goes stale the direction has failed at the thing it exists to do.

Fixed versus customisable

Direction-fixed: the status panel as hero, no image above the fold, fee stated in the first sentence, the "last checked" date, the compact scale, sans-only type, the short enquiry form and its health-details microcopy.
Client-customisable: the accent within three tested alternatives to teal, all words, the place photographs, every value in the status panel, whether a sliding scale is shown as a range or a single figure.

Website system

Sitemap: Home · What to expect · Fees and how it works · Where to find us · Contact · Privacy · Accessibility · 404. Six visible pages.

Homepage order: (1) Name, town, one sentence including the fee. (2) The status panel — fee, session length, location, online option, current availability, "last checked" date. (3) Who this is for and who it is not for, in two short lists. (4) What happens when you get in touch, three steps with real timings. (5) Where to find us, with the photograph and transport and access notes. (6) Enquiry. (7) Urgent-support boundary, which for this direction should also signpost genuinely free options, because some readers will not be able to afford even this.

Components: status panel, availability notice, fee range block, who-it-is-and-isn't-for lists, step sequence, access and transport block, short enquiry form, urgent-support panel with free alternatives, registration placeholder, concept notice.

Calls to action: one, repeated top and bottom of every page — "Ask about a first session".

SEO: the highest-intent search behaviour of the six. "Low cost counselling [town]", "counselling [town] £", "cheap therapy near me" are searched with immediate intent. Strong local signals matter here more than anywhere: a genuine address, consistent name-address-phone, and a Google Business Profile. Do not promise rankings; do build the foundations and say which are the client's to maintain.

Mobile priorities: the whole status panel must fit one phone screen without scrolling — that constraint sets how many rows it may have. The primary action is reachable with one thumb at the top of the page. Total page weight under 250KB, because this reader is more likely to be on a poor connection or a metered plan than any other in the library.

Trust and factual checklist: fee stated in the first sentence · sliding scale explained if offered · session length stated · address and transport stated · online option stated · current availability with a real "last checked" date · who it is not for, stated honestly · registration placeholder present · urgent-support boundary including free alternatives · privacy notice · no testimonials · no treatment claims · concept disclosure on every page.

Content required from the client: the least of the six — around 450 words, a set of facts, and two photographs of the actual place. The real commitment is maintenance: the status panel must be updated, and the direction should be sold with that said out loud.

Commercial fit

Straightforward Website. Deliberately the cheapest of the six to deliver: fewest words, most fixed structure, least editorial judgement. That matters because this direction's buyers have the least money — a newly qualified therapist or a low-fee service cannot fund a Guided engagement, and pricing it as though they could would mean the direction never sells. It also pairs naturally with a care plan, since the status panel is the one thing that genuinely needs maintaining.

The compliance layer, built once

Every direction inherits the same five obligations, drawn from the evidence dossier rather than invented:

  • Registration placeholder that cannot be filled loosely. The schema takes a body, a category and a number, and renders only the exact permitted string. BACP prescribes its wording precisely, and in March 2025 the ASA ruled against a UK counselling practice for saying "accredited by" when the therapist was registered — a complaint brought by BACP itself. A free-text field invites exactly that error.
  • No testimonials component exists in the kit. UKCP's 2019 Code, clause 13, prohibits client testimonials in advertising outright. Building the component and leaving it unused would mean a UKCP-registered client could switch it on.
  • Copy rules enforced at review, not by the framework. CAP rule 12.2 bars claims to "diagnose, treat, help with or cure" without appropriate qualification and evidence. "Helps with anxiety" is defensible; "treats anxiety" is not. This belongs in a one-page sheet handed to every client, not in code.
  • Enquiry forms that minimise special-category data. A message box inviting someone to describe what brings them to therapy collects health data under UK GDPR Article 9 the moment it is answered. Every direction's form carries microcopy asking people not to include clinical detail yet, and links the client's own privacy notice at the point of submission.
  • Urgent-support boundary on every page of every direction. Never styled as an alarm, never omitted, and in two directions — Sounding and Two Chairs — carrying additional specificity that the others do not need.

Deliverability

A direction is realistically finished when it passes the shared harness at four viewports, hits the Core Web Vitals thresholds on a mid-range Android over 4G, carries every item on its own trust checklist, and reads correctly in greyscale. That is a testable definition, and it is the same for all six — which is what "consistently refined over time" actually requires.

Questions to settle before any code

Twelve. The first four are blocking; the rest can be answered as the first direction is built, but not later than that.

Blocking

  1. Astro 5 or Astro 7? Two concepts on each. The kit cannot span both, and whichever loses needs its concepts migrated or retired.
  2. One monorepo, or a repository per direction? This determines whether a fix to the urgent-support panel reaches six sites or one, and it is very hard to change later.
  3. One hosting provider — which? Netlify, Cloudflare Pages and Cloudflare Workers are all in use across the estate. The Worker in Rowan Hill is the outlier and needs a decision either way.
  4. Does a client edit their own content, and through what? The answer changes the content-collection design fundamentally, and it is the question a buyer will ask in the first conversation.

Needed during the first build

  1. Enquiry-form handling. Still open on the studio site itself. Real server-side submission means special-category data in transit and a processor relationship; the current browser-only approach avoids that and loses enquiries. Every direction inherits whichever answer you give.
  2. Fonts: @fontsource self-hosted, or Google Fonts? Self-hosting is the licensing-clean answer at handover, but it adds a build step to every direction. Six of the nine faces proposed here are SIL Open Font License, which makes self-hosting straightforward — worth confirming the remaining three before committing.
  3. Is the concept disclosure required on all six demo sites? Your published rule says yes; six of seven current concepts do not carry it. Confirm the rule, then apply it retrospectively.
  4. Is the fee published by default in every direction? The evidence says omitting it creates hesitation rather than removing it. But it is the client's call, and the directions differ: Noticeboard cannot work without it, Sounding barely needs it above the fold.

Commercial, and needed before the collection is published

  1. How many practices may buy each direction, and is the number stated publicly? This is what makes "not a template" structurally true rather than a claim. It also needs a rule about geography and specialism.
  2. Photography: who supplies it, and what happens when the client has none? Four of the six directions depend on a real photograph of a real place. The prompts here are placeholders, and placeholders that ship are a known failure mode.
  3. Are the fictional worked examples published as live demo sites? If yes, they need the concept-safety rules applied in full — noindex, no sitemap, no real domain or contact details, inert forms — and the studio needs to be comfortable that six invented practices are visible under its name.
  4. What happens to Harbour, North & Vale and Stillpoint? They are outside these six. Kept as portfolio evidence, refined later, or retired — but they should not sit in the collection implying they are buyable if they are not.
