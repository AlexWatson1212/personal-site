What you are actually buying

One piece of work, and the first part is the point.

Most therapist websites start with the website. I start with the practice: who you work well with, how you think about therapy, what matters to you, and what somebody needs to understand before they contact you. You are paying for that understanding, the visual identity that comes out of it, and a finished website that is recognisably yours.

Each part decides the next, which is why they are not sold separately and why you are never asked to choose how much of one you need.

  1. 01 · Your intake

    You tell me about the practice through one intake form — in rough notes, or voice notes if that is easier, over as many sittings as you like. The practical facts are asked for exactly; nothing is guessed.

  2. 02 · Practice Fundamentals

    I work it into your Practice Fundamentals: eleven pages covering your position, practical facts, beliefs and boundaries, descriptions at several lengths, voice and vocabulary, your logo or wordmark, colours, typography and photography direction, with a working preview of your website’s opening screen.

  3. 03 · Approve the direction

    You check the facts and either approve the direction or send one consolidated set of adjustments. This is the first of two feedback stages, and the decision that matters most: nothing is built until you have approved it.

  4. 04 · Build, review and launch

    One complete, responsive page, built from the direction you approved. You send one consolidated set of feedback on it, and I make the changes, connect your domain and launch, with the first year of Practice Care. You keep the Practice Fundamentals and the files.

A template solves part of the last stage and nothing else. It gives your words somewhere to go, and for some practices that is genuinely the right purchase. What it cannot do is work out what the practice should say, what to emphasise, what to remove and what not to claim — and those decisions are what the design is downstream of. Nor does it leave you holding anything once it is finished. That is the difference the price is for, rather than the fact that somebody knows how to build web pages.

Practice Fundamentals

Understanding the practice is the work. It is not an extra.

Every website starts here. You are not asked to work out how much of it you need, or to buy the right amount of thinking — that judgement is mine, and it is most of what the {{ site.data.purchasing.price_display }} pays for.

For a practice you can already describe, it is mostly listening, and then deciding: what to lead with, what to cut, what a visitor needs first. For a practice that is still settling, it asks more questions. Either way it happens before anything is built, and what comes out of it is written down for you to approve.

The reason it is not sold separately is that a website will not resolve an unresolved practice — it will only display the uncertainty more attractively. If I think the work needed is larger than a website project can carry, I will say so before anything starts rather than building on top of it.

What you receive is your Practice Fundamentals: eleven pages recording what I have understood about the practice and the decisions that follow from it — your position, the facts, what you believe and will not claim, how the practice is described at several lengths, how it sounds and how it looks — with a working preview of your website’s opening screen. You check the facts and approve the direction, or send one consolidated set of adjustments, and nothing is built until you have approved it.

The three documents in the collection show the same kind of thinking, published in full as Practice Clarity Blueprints. They belong to fictional practices and were written after their concept sites; yours is written for your practice, before anything is built.

  1. Practice

    What is actually being offered here, in plain terms, and what is not?

  2. People

    Who is this for — and, just as usefully, who is it not for?

  3. Position

    What is distinctive about it, said without claiming anything you cannot stand behind?

  4. Message

    What should somebody understand within seconds, and what language fits you well enough to say out loud?

  5. Expression

    How the practice should feel, what to emphasise, what to remove, and how the services are organised.

Those five are what your Practice Fundamentals settles, and the website is built from it. The document is yours to keep, with a quick check for anything you write later, so the next time you write a directory profile or a leaflet there is something to work from. What that means in practice.

What it is not: business or marketing consultancy, advice on your fees, or a plan for getting more clients. It settles how the practice should be described. What you do with the practice itself stays yours.

Questions I am asked most

Do I own the website?

Yes. On full payment the finished website made for your practice is yours to keep, host and move, and your domain stays in your own account. The underlying design system stays mine, licensed to you permanently for one website for your own practice.

What if I change my mind about the look?

The look is settled in your Practice Fundamentals, and the first feedback stage is the point to shape it, before anything is built. The second stage, on the built website, is for refining and correcting it. A different look after you have approved the direction is a new brief rather than feedback, and is quoted.

Can you write it for me?

Yes. The words on your website are written from your intake — your answers, your own phrases, your voice notes — so they still sound like you, and they are set out in your Practice Fundamentals for you to check before anything is built. You do not need to arrive with finished copy. Writing for pages beyond the one included, or for articles, is quoted separately.

I already have a website. What happens to it?

Your domain is yours and stays in your own account throughout. If it is registered somewhere else, I help you point it at the new website rather than taking it over, and that help continues through the first year of Care.

Point me to your current site in the intake and I will use whatever is worth keeping. The new website is one complete page, built with the same essential on-page SEO as every website here: a title, a description, headings, alt text and a sitemap.

What I will not do is tell you what will happen to your position in search results, because nobody can honestly promise that. If your current site has several pages, or pages that other people link to, say so when you write: whether one page is still right, and what happens to those addresses, is settled in writing along with the rest of the scope, before anything is agreed.

If you would rather keep the website you have and only need one thing on it sorted, that is help with your existing website, quoted on its own.

Is one page really enough?

For most private practices, yes. One carefully designed page can hold who you are, how you work, your fees, the practical information and an enquiry form, and it is easier for somebody nervous about getting in touch to take in at once.

If you need several pages, online booking, a blog or unusual functionality, say so when you write. It is quoted separately, and if I think one page will not give you what you need, I will tell you before anything is agreed.

Will you follow my professional body's rules?

Designations are set exactly as BACP, UKCP, NCPS or your own body permits, and nothing goes on the page you cannot stand behind. I do not give regulatory advice, and the published website remains your responsibility.

I am not technical. Is that a problem?

No. The intake is in plain language, the design decisions are mine, and if you would rather not touch it at all, that is what Care is for.

Can I edit the website myself?

No, and it is worth being plain about it. There is no login, no page builder and no admin area. That is the reason the website is fast, stays accessible, and has nothing to keep updated or broken into — but it does mean the changes go through me.

In practice: for the first thirty days after launch, faults in what I delivered are corrected free. Practice Care — the first year is included — keeps the website working and covers genuine technical faults. Changing your fee, your availability or a paragraph, or adding a section or a page, is new work: {{ site.data.purchasing.hourly_rate }} an hour, or a fixed quote where the scope is clear, agreed in writing before I do it.

If holding the keys yourself matters to you, say so when you write. Some practices genuinely want that, and I will tell you honestly that I am not the right person for it rather than sell you something you will find frustrating.

Can I pause, or get a refund?

A project that goes quiet is held for sixty days, then treated as dormant. Refunds depend on how far the work has gone; the position is set out in full on the cancellations and refunds page.

Is this a template?

No, and it is worth being exact about the difference rather than defensive about it. A template solves layout, structure and styling. It gives your words somewhere to go, and for some practices that is the right purchase.

It cannot decide who the practice is for, what should be said, what should not be claimed, or what a particular reader needs before they will make contact. Those decisions are the work here, and the design comes out of them — which is why the three in the collection have almost nothing in common rather than being one layout in three colourways. Five of those decisions, with the reasoning.

How is this different from a bespoke website?

It is designed for your practice, so in that sense it is bespoke. What it is not is open-ended: one direction, worked out in your Practice Fundamentals and approved by you, and one complete page, rather than several competing concepts to choose between. That is what keeps it at a fixed, published price.
