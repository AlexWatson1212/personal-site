What you are actually buying

One piece of work in three stages, not three things bought together.

You are not paying for pages to be built. You are paying for the work of taking a practice that is difficult to describe — which most good practices are — and making it coherent, recognisable and easy for the right person to trust.

Each stage decides the next, which is why they are not sold separately and why you are never asked to choose how much of one you need.

  1. Practice Clarity · the thinking

    Who the practice is for and who it is not for, what is actually being offered, what should be led with, what should be left out, and what a particular reader needs before they will make contact. Written down and approved before anything is designed.

  2. Identity · the visual expression

    Typography, colour, hierarchy and a simple wordmark where one helps — chosen because of what the first stage settled, not because they look well together. This is the identity the website needs rather than a brand programme, and the design decisions in it are mine to make.

  3. The website · the implementation

    Structure and information architecture, your words set properly, a responsive and accessible build, essential on-page SEO, an enquiry route that suits a therapy practice, two consolidated rounds of changes, launch, handover and the first year of care.

A template solves the third stage and only the third stage. It gives your words somewhere to go, and for some practices that is genuinely the right purchase. What it cannot do is work out what the practice should say, what to emphasise, what to remove and what not to claim — and those decisions are what the design is downstream of. That is the difference the price is for, rather than the fact that somebody knows how to build web pages.

Practice Clarity

Understanding the practice is the work. It is not an extra.

Every website includes as much of this as it needs. You are not asked to work out how much that is, or to buy the right amount of thinking — that judgement is mine, and it is most of what the {{ site.data.purchasing.price_display }} pays for.

For a practice you can already describe, it is mostly listening, and then deciding: what to lead with, what to cut, what a visitor needs first. For a practice that is still settling, it takes longer and asks more questions. Either way it happens before anything is designed, and what comes out of it is written down for you to approve.

The reason it is not sold separately is that a website will not resolve an unresolved practice — it will only display the uncertainty more attractively. If I think the work needed is larger than a website project can carry, I will say so before anything starts rather than building on top of it.

What you receive from it is the Direction Note: what I have understood, who the site is speaking to, and the decisions I am proposing — before anything is built, with one round of changes included. It is the same work as the documents below, at the length a project needs rather than the length a case study can afford.

The six documents in the collection show this method in unusually full form, because a portfolio case has room to publish its whole argument. What a client receives is shorter: the decisions that govern their website, written down.

  1. Practice

    What is actually being offered here, in plain terms, and what is not?

  2. People

    Who is this for — and, just as usefully, who is it not for?

  3. Position

    What is distinctive about it, said without claiming anything you cannot stand behind?

  4. Message

    What should somebody understand within seconds, and what language fits you well enough to say out loud?

  5. Expression

    How the practice should feel, what to emphasise, what to remove, and how the services are organised.

Those five are what the Direction Note records, and the website is built from it. The answers are useful beyond the website too — on a directory profile, in a referral conversation, on a leaflet — and they are yours to use that way.

What it is not: business or marketing consultancy, advice on your fees, or a plan for getting more clients. It settles how the practice should be described. What you do with the practice itself stays yours.

Questions I am asked most

Do I own the website?

Yes. On full payment the finished website made for your practice is yours to keep, host and move, and your domain stays in your own account. The underlying design system stays mine, licensed to you permanently for one website for your own practice.

What if I change my mind about the look?

Before the intake is complete, one change of visual direction is free. After the build has started it is a new brief rather than a revision, and is quoted.

Can you write it for me?

I edit and shape what you write, which for most therapists is the right amount of help: the words still sound like you. Working out who the practice is for and what needs saying is included, so you do not have to arrive with that settled. Full copywriting from a blank page is outside the normal website scope.

I already have a website. What happens to it?

Your domain is yours and stays in your own account throughout. If it is registered somewhere else, I help you point it at the new website rather than taking it over, and that help continues through the first year of Care.

Send me the words and images from your current site and I will edit and shape whatever is worth keeping — that is included. Writing from a blank page is not, and I will say so rather than quietly absorbing it. The new website is built with the same essential on-page SEO as every website here: titles, descriptions, headings, alt text and a sitemap.

What I will not do is tell you what will happen to your position in search results, because nobody can honestly promise that. If your current site has pages that other people link to, say so when you write: what happens to those addresses is settled in writing along with the rest of the scope, before anything is agreed.

Will you follow my professional body's rules?

Designations are set exactly as BACP, UKCP, NCPS or your own body permits, and nothing goes on the page you cannot stand behind. I do not give regulatory advice, and the published website remains your responsibility.

I am not technical. Is that a problem?

No. The intake is in plain language, the design decisions are mine, and if you would rather not touch it at all, that is what Care is for.

Can I edit the website myself?

No, and it is worth being plain about it. There is no login, no page builder and no admin area. That is the reason the website is fast, stays accessible, and has nothing to keep updated or broken into — but it does mean the changes go through me.

In practice: for the first thirty days after launch, corrections are free. After that, changing your fee, your availability, a paragraph of your biography or your contact details is an email to me, quoted before I do it and usually small. A new page or a new section is a new piece of work, quoted the same way.

If holding the keys yourself matters to you, say so when you write. Some practices genuinely want that, and I will tell you honestly that I am not the right person for it rather than sell you something you will find frustrating.

Can I pause, or get a refund?

A project that goes quiet is held for sixty days, then treated as dormant. Refunds depend on how far the work has gone; the position is set out in full on the cancellations and refunds page.

Is this a template?

No, and it is worth being exact about the difference rather than defensive about it. A template solves layout, structure and styling. It gives your words somewhere to go, and for some practices that is the right purchase.

It cannot decide who the practice is for, what should be said, what should not be claimed, or what a particular reader needs before they will make contact. Those decisions are the work here, and the design comes out of them — which is why the six in the collection have almost nothing in common rather than being one layout in six colourways. Five of those decisions, with the reasoning.

How is this different from a bespoke website?

The visual exploration has already happened. The thinking about your practice still happens, with every client. What you are buying is that thinking, the tailoring, the build and the care — not three weeks of concept work.
