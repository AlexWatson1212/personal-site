# Legal review pack — Alexander Watson Studio

**For a UK commercial solicitor. Prepared 27 August 2026.**

This document exists so a review can be scoped and priced quickly. The
contractual documents are already drafted and published in draft form; what is
needed is judgement on the specific points below, and drafting where the answer
requires it.

**Nothing in this pack is a legal opinion.** Every question is open. Where a
commercial intention is stated, it is an intention and not reviewed wording — the
published pages must not be redrafted to assert it until that wording has been
settled.

## How the documents currently behave

Every legal page renders a visible notice reading *"Draft — requires review by a
UK commercial solicitor before live use"*, and unfilled facts render as visible
`[square bracket]` placeholders rather than plausible defaults. Both are
controlled by one flag, `approved` in `_data/legal.yml`. Setting it to `true`
removes the notices from every page at once, and it will be set only when a
review has actually happened.

Passages most in need of review are marked ⚖ in the page bodies.

## 1. The current intended commercial structure

One product with one optional add-on. No tiers, no packages, no bundles.

**Therapist Website — £995 fixed.** The client chooses one of a set of complete
design directions produced by the studio; the studio tailors it to her practice
and builds, launches and hands over the finished website. Included: a written
intake, a Direction Note (see §7), a tailored website of up to five core pages,
two consolidated revision rounds, accessibility and performance checks, launch,
handover, a thirty-day correction period, and the first twelve months of Website
Care.

**Practice Clarity — £500.** A separate, earlier piece of work producing a short
written Practice Direction the client keeps whether or not a website follows.
Agreed in writing and invoiced separately. Offered only where the intake shows it
is needed. Never combined with £995 into a single headline figure.

**Website Care.** Included for twelve months, then optional at £29 per month with
no minimum term. It covers hosting, TLS renewal, deployments, dependency and
security updates, version history, domain and DNS help, and fixing faults in the
website or its deployment. It expressly does **not** claim backups, guaranteed
uptime or continuous monitoring, none of which are operated.

**Custom projects.** Scoped and quoted individually, outside this structure.

**The buying route is deliberately human.** At launch there is no self-service
checkout. The client names a design; the studio confirms the scope and price in
writing; a payment link for the first instalment is sent directly; the
questionnaire follows. **Question: on these facts, which document forms the
contract and at what moment?** The general terms currently contain a clause about
which published price is an offer capable of acceptance, drafted when a checkout
was contemplated. It needs rereading against the written route.

## 2. The £500 / £495 payment model

£995 in total, in two instalments:

| | Amount | When |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First instalment | £500 | On acceptance, before the project begins |
| Balance | £495 | On the client's explicit written approval of the finished website, before it is made live |

Two features are deliberate and should be reflected in the wording:

- The balance is triggered by **the client's written approval**, not by the
  studio declaring the work finished.
- The project **officially begins** later than payment: once the completed
  questionnaire and materials have been received and checked and a start date has
  been confirmed in writing. Payment alone does not start the build.

**Questions.**

a. Does the instalment structure need anything the current clause 3 does not say?
b. Is the "project officially begins" construction sound, given that money has
   changed hands before it?
c. If the client does not approve the finished website, the intention is that the
   balance is not payable and she may take the work as it stands. Is that
   enforceable and is it wise?

## 3. Intended licence outcomes

These are the studio's commercial intentions. **They are not drafted wording and
must not be published as though they were.**

1. The licence to the delivered website is **perpetual**. It does not end if
   Website Care ends.
2. The client **may appoint another developer** to maintain or modify that
   website.
3. The licence covers **that one website for that practice**. A second website
   requires a new licence.
4. **Resale or redistribution of the underlying design system is not permitted.**

Background: the client's own copy, images, logo and practice information are
hers; the finished customised website is hers on receipt of full payment; the
underlying design system, layout, code and studio-created design assets remain
the studio's and are licensed to her. The studio may show the finished website in
its portfolio unless asked in writing not to.

**Questions.**

a. Please draft clause 13 to achieve outcomes 1–4.
b. Does outcome 2 create any risk the studio should be protected against — for
   example, liability for a third party's later modifications?
c. Does "on receipt of full payment" work sensibly with the two-instalment model,
   given that the balance falls due at approval and before launch?

## 4. Cancellation and refunds

The published page currently sets out a stage-by-stage position. **A revised
ladder has been designed but deliberately not published**, because it depends on
assumptions about consumer status and the treatment of work already undertaken
that are not the studio's to make.

The intended commercial shape, for review rather than for adoption:

| Point reached | Paid | Intended outcome |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Before the start date is confirmed | £500 | Refund, less any payment processing fee not returned |
| After the start, before the Direction Note | £500 | Proportionate retention for work carried out, with the figure shown and explained |
| After the Direction Note is delivered | £500 | No refund; the client holds a completed deliverable and nothing further is due |
| After the first version is delivered | £500 | No refund; the balance is not payable; the work may be taken as it stands |
| After launch | £995 | Not refundable; thirty days of corrections; statutory rights unaffected |

**Questions.**

a. Is this ladder lawful and expressible, for a consumer and for a business
   purchaser respectively?
b. Is "the client holds a completed deliverable" a sound basis for retaining the
   first instalment at that stage?
c. What must be said about statutory rights alongside it?

## 5. Consumer or business classification

A therapist in private practice buying a website may be acting wholly or mainly
outside her trade or profession, or within it. The studio's current position is
that it does not assume either, and asks the client to state which applies.

**Questions.**

a. Is refusing to assume the right approach, or does it create more difficulty
   than it avoids?
b. What should the studio ask, and how should the answer be recorded?
c. If a purchaser is a consumer, which parts of the current documents change?

## 8. Limitation of liability

placeholder.

Relevant facts: the studio is a one-person business; the fee is £995; the studio
hosts the client's website during Website Care; the client's website may be the
principal route by which her clients find her; the studio does not verify

**Questions.**

a. What cap is appropriate and enforceable at this fee level?
b. What must not be excluded?
c. Is the division of responsibility for the published content — the client

## 10. Website Care and continuing obligations

Twelve months of Website Care are included inside the £995 rather than sold
separately. After that it continues at £29 per month if the client wants it, with
no minimum term, ending at the end of the month in which she says stop. There is
no subscription infrastructure: no recurring checkout, no stored card, no
automatic renewal. The studio undertakes to write before the twelfth month and
ask.

Care has no terms of its own.

**Questions.**

a. Does twelve months of included hosting and maintenance create a continuing
   obligation that ought to be described differently from the way it is now?
b. Does Care need its own terms before it is charged for?
c. Is anything owed to a client whose Care ends — for example, assistance moving
   the website elsewhere? The studio intends to provide what is needed to move
   it, and would like that intention properly expressed.

## 12. What changed on 5 September 2026, and why

The public pages were corrected before this pack was sent, so that what a solicitor
reads matches what a visitor reads. Nothing below is a legal judgement; each item
removed a statement the site was making about itself that was not true.

**Contract formation (service terms clause 4).** The clause said acceptance was
"recorded at the Stripe checkout" and that Stripe "requires you to confirm that you
accept these terms before your payment can be completed". No self-service checkout has
ever existed, and clause 3 said so in the same document. Clause 4 now describes the route
that operates: scope and price confirmed by email, that email referring to these terms,
and a contract formed on written acceptance and payment of the first instalment.
**This is the item most in need of review** — whether formation sits at written
acceptance, at payment, or at both, is a question for you rather than for us, and the
wording has been kept deliberately plain so it can be replaced.

**General terms.** "They are shown and accepted at checkout" and "Nothing on any page of
this website is an offer capable of acceptance, except the Therapist Website checkout"
both described the same non-existent mechanism. The second sentence had the effect, read
literally, that nothing on the site could be accepted at all. Both now describe the
written route.

**Privacy notice section 2.** Was headed "Stripe checkout" and listed the fields
collected "at present" at a checkout that does not exist. Stripe remains the processor —
a payment link is sent after the scope is agreed — so the section is now headed "Payment"
and describes what Stripe collects to take a payment and issue a receipt.

**Cancellation and refunds.** The page answered the same scenario twice and differently:
"you may cancel within that period and receive a full refund" in the consumer section,
and "a full refund will be made, less any payment processing fee that Stripe does not
return to me" in the stage-by-stage section. There is now one answer, and it is the one
that cannot disadvantage a buyer: a full refund with nothing deducted. **Please confirm
whether any deduction is lawful on a consumer cancellation, and whether a different
position is available on a business purchase.** The same sentence on the Therapist
Website page was corrected to match.

**Website Care renewal.** The sales page said "I will write to you before the year is up
and ask"; clause 12 said Care "continues only if you ask for it". Those are different
obligations. Both now say that the Studio writes to ask and that Care continues only on
the client's yes.

**Handover.** Clause 12 promised to "hand over what is needed to host it elsewhere"
without saying what that was. It now lists what is provided. The internal source is
`docs/handover-runbook.md`; the two must not drift apart.

**Three bracketed notes were removed from public pages.** They were instructions to you,
not information for a reader, and a client reading the terms should not be reading them.
Nothing was lost — each is already a question in this pack: the liability cap (§8), the
model cancellation form (§9), and an alternative dispute resolution route (§11). Clause
16 now states plainly that no cap is claimed, which is the conservative position pending
your drafting.

**Still unfilled, and deliberately so.** Eleven factual placeholders remain visible on
the public pages, driven from `_data/legal.yml`. They are listed in
`LEGAL-INFORMATION-REQUIRED.md`. They are Alexander's to supply and were not guessed.
