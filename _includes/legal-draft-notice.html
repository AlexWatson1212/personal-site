{%- comment -%} The status note at the top of a legal page. September 2026. This used to be an internal development label: it announced "Draft — requires review by a UK commercial solicitor before live use", said the document was "not yet in force", and explained the ⚖ flags and the [square bracket] placeholders. All of that is true and none of it was written for the person it is shown to. A therapist being asked to accept these terms and transfer the first instalment reads "draft" and "not in force" and reasonably concludes there is no agreement — which is the opposite of what the page is for, and the single most confidence-damaging thing on the purchase route. docs/pre-launch.md §5 records the decision to replace it. What has NOT changed: site.data.legal.approved still means "a solicitor has reviewed this wording", it is still false, and the notice below still says so plainly. The claim being dropped is that the document is not in force, not the admission that it is unreviewed. Nothing here may describe the wording as legally approved, legally checked or professionally drafted while that flag is false — that would be a false statement about a real professional service. The ⚖ flags and the [square bracket] placeholders are unchanged and still render. They no longer need explaining to a client: a bracket that says [business address] explains itself, and the flags are for the solicitor.
