Most therapists write their website as though every visitor is a
potential client. It is an understandable instinct, especially when the
diary is not full. It also produces the exact problem people complain
about: a steady trickle of enquiries that are not a fit, each of which
costs a considerate reply.

The website that produces fewer, better enquiries is the one that is
willing to be specific enough to lose people.

## What "put off" actually means here

Not rude. Not exclusionary. Not a list of who you will not see.

It means the page says clearly enough what you do that somebody looking
for something else can tell, quickly, and go and find it. That is a
service to them. Nobody wants to send a careful, difficult first email
and receive a polite referral elsewhere.

## Where specificity usually goes missing

**In who you work with.** "I work with adults experiencing a wide range
of difficulties" is true of almost every therapist alive. It gives the
reader nothing to recognise. Compare: *I mostly work with people in
their thirties and forties who are functioning well on the outside and
finding it increasingly expensive to keep it up.* Some readers will
recognise themselves in that sentence in a way they never will in the
first.

**In how you work.** Long-term, open-ended, exploratory work is a
different offer from eight sessions with a focus. Say which. A person
who wants six weeks of practical help and lands with a psychodynamic
practitioner has wasted both your times.

**In the practicalities.** Daytime only. Online only. No, I do not offer
same-week appointments. Waiting list of about a month. Each of these
loses you somebody, and each of them loses you somebody who was going to
be disappointed anyway.

## The awkward one: fees

Publishing your fee is the clearest example of a page doing this well.

It removes the enquiries you cannot serve, before they cost either of
you anything. It also signals that you are comfortable with what you
charge, which is read — accurately — as a sign of an established
practice.

If your fee is at the higher end, saying so plainly does more good than
concealing it. Somebody who cannot afford it will find that out
eventually. Better at the moment of scanning than three emails in.

## What it does not mean

It does not mean inventing a niche you do not have. A great many
therapists genuinely do work across a broad range, and pretending
otherwise produces a website that misrepresents the practice — which is
worse than a general one.

If you are genuinely general, be specific about something else: how you
work, who you are, what a first session is like, what you are good at.
There is always something concrete to say.

## The reframe

You are not trying to reach the most people. You are trying to be
recognised by the right ones.

A therapist's diary holds a small number of people. It does not need a
large audience. It needs the twenty or thirty people a year for whom
you are a good fit to be able to find you and know it.

Specificity is how they know it.
