On a phone

Most of the people reading are on a phone, and some of them are not calm.

The phone view is not the desktop one stacked up: the hierarchy is re-decided, the type re-set, and the thing a distressed reader needs first moved to where they will find it. Every direction is tested to WCAG 2.2 AA.

Taken all the way

Six of them have the reasoning published beside the design.

Six finished concept websites for six fictional practices, each published live with the strategic work that produced it. They are deliberately nothing like each other. What they share is that every decision on the page is set against the conclusion it came from, so you can check whether the second actually explains the first.

Sofia Marin

Therapy for people who are tired of being read accurately.

A fictional integrative therapist in Brighton, for adults whose lives are working and no longer feel like theirs. The site describes nobody and hands them the method instead: one sentence that never changes, and the meanings underneath it that do.

See the Sofia Marin case →

Maya Bennett

Counselling for the part before anybody has decided it counts.

A fictional integrative relational counsellor in Bristol. The site removes the preconditions people invent before counselling feels legitimate, then argues that the unprepared account is the more useful material to start from.

See the Maya Bennett case →

Daniel Mercer

Counselling for the person who assumes he would be bad at therapy.

A fictional integrative counsellor in Sheffield, for adults whose way of coping has quietly stopped working. Most therapist websites promise an outcome; this one removes an entry requirement, and answers every fear with a fact rather than a reassurance.

See the Daniel Mercer case →

Helen Calder

An active therapist who does not take authority over the conclusion.

A fictional integrative counsellor in Didsbury, for adults who have already spent years thinking about something alone. The site says what she will actually do in the hour — ask, notice, name the awkward thing — and is equally clear that the conclusions stay theirs.

See the Helen Calder case →

Harbour

An organisation whose whole proposition is the removal of ambush.

A fictional therapy centre in Stroud — five people, one building and four readerships who need contradictory things. Nothing arrives without notice and almost nothing proceeds without your say-so, which is why the site routes before it describes anything at all.

See the Harbour case →

Stillpoint

Nothing required in advance, and everything disclosed in advance.

A fictional counsellor in Lewes, for people who are managing perfectly well and have run out of anywhere to say the thing. The quietest site in the collection, and the most concrete about fees, days and access — which turns out to be the same decision twice.

See the Stillpoint case →
