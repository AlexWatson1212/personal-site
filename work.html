What every case has behind it

A live website, and the reasoning that produced it.

Each of the six is deployed and can be visited, page by page, on a phone if you want to see it there. Each has a strategic document published in full and free to read, in which every design decision is set against the conclusion it came from. Where a document and a build disagreed, the build changed and the document says so.

Most of the people arriving on a therapist’s website are on a phone, and some of them are not calm. The phone view of each of these is not the desktop one stacked up: the hierarchy is re-decided, the type re-set, and the thing a distressed reader needs first is moved to where they will find it. All six are built to WCAG 2.2 AA.

Stillpoint · page fourteen of seventeen, “Website translation”.

Your method is subtraction: you take out whatever would make a client perform.

The requirement set beside it: the interface subtracts too, and the bands carry a single sentence without apologising for the space.

No cards, icon sets, testimonials, badges, pop-ups, chat widget or newsletter prompt. Long intervals between sections, and one typeface doing all of the work.

Why is it like that?

Five small decisions, and what each one came from.

None of these is a visual preference. Each is something understood about a practice, arriving somewhere different on a page — which is why the same question, asked of six practices, does not produce six versions of one website.

Colour and type

Why does Stillpoint never use colour to emphasise anything?

Because its counsellor will not tell anybody how their grief or their divorce is supposed to feel. Colour carries no mood: one accent, used only for what can be clicked, and no photograph brought in to supply an emotion the copy has declined to name.

Stillpoint · Practice Clarity Blueprint, website translation
The first screen

Why is the fee on the first screen of Daniel Mercer’s site?

Because the design is asked to carry no atmosphere at all, so everything the visitor is meant to feel has to arrive as information: what it costs, what happens, and what he will not do. The fee is repeated in the first screen, the navigation and a fixed bar on mobile.

Daniel Mercer · Practice Clarity Blueprint, philosophy of practice
What gets published

Why does Helen Calder’s site publish the width of her doorway?

Because trust in this practice is earned by volunteering the awkward fact. The cancellation terms, the reply time, the waiting list, the limits of confidentiality, what she does not offer, and the 76cm doorway are all written as content at full size — each stated once, so repetition does not spend the effect.

Helen Calder · Practice Clarity Blueprint, website translation
The order of the page

Why does Harbour route you somewhere before it describes anything?

Because four readerships with different fears cannot be served by one page. Routing happens before any service description is attempted: four named routes in the first screen, in a fixed order, held at equal weight so that none of them reads as the children’s part of the site.

Harbour · Practice Clarity Blueprint, website translation
The enquiry form

Why does Sofia Marin’s form not ask what the problem is?

Because the first message she wants is “I’m not sure how to describe this.” So there is no field asking for the problem: a name, a way to reply, in person or online, and one optional box whose label says that exact sentence is a complete answer.

Sofia Marin · Practice Clarity Blueprint, intended client experience
