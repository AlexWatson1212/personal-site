# Instagram — content direction (internal)

Recorded 29 September 2026, when Post One was finalised. Not a calendar. Nothing here is a finished post.

## Working principle

**Publish, observe, learn, then make the next thing.**

One feed post at a time. Before each one, look at what's been published, what people responded to, DMs and questions, profile reviews done, and any new real-world evidence. Then decide what's genuinely worth saying next. The account stays mine: counselling, learning, personal posts and projects carry on alongside these threads. Stories carry the lighter, more frequent interaction (questions, polls, work in progress, the offer to look at someone's website or profile) so the feed doesn't need a CTA on everything.

## Thread one: understanding the studio

Anchor subjects that should appear naturally over time, not as consecutive campaign posts:

- what I've been building and why (Post One)
- different parts of a therapist's practice created separately: each fine, none connected
- what I mean by professional identity: clarity first, visual decisions after, not a logo or elaborate branding
- one concept practice in depth, always labelled fictional
- the same process producing three very different practices
- eventually: the actual service, the process, what someone receives, and the £495 founding offer and why it exists

## Thread two: what I notice when I look at therapist practices

An ongoing series that shows how I think rather than repeating what I sell. Possible territory, **only when a real observation exists**:

- what an About page needs to help a prospective client understand
- patterns across therapist directory profiles
- why a website and a Counselling Directory profile needn't match word for word but should feel like the same practice
- cases where the first thing I'd change isn't the design
- therapists with similar modalities who still need different identities and websites
- therapy language that's accurate but tells a prospective client very little
- things noticed in free profile reviews
- real questions therapists ask me
- decisions from client work, with permission and appropriate confidentiality

## Evidence rule

Don't manufacture observations. Material should increasingly come from profile reviews, conversations, DMs, websites actually reviewed, questions asked, client work and my own developing understanding. Content should get more evidence-based as the studio develops, not less. Concept work is always described as fictional. Counselling training is context, never authority.
