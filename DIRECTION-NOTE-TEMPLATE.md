# The Direction Note

A named deliverable inside the £995 Therapist Website. Sent within five working
days of a complete intake, and approved in writing before the build starts.

**It answers:** *Has Alexander understood me, and am I comfortable with where he
intends to take this?*

**It does not answer:** *Is every element of my finished website exactly where I
want it?* That is what the two revision rounds are for.

One consolidated revision of the Note is included, and it is separate from the
two website revision rounds.

---

## Format

- One landscape page, 1440 × 810pt, on the studio document system.
- Newsreader (Display 72pt optical above 24pt, Text below) and Instrument Sans.
- Studio palette. Per-client variation is confined to the swatch band in §5.
- Delivered as a PDF attached to an email whose body carries the approval
  question in plain text, so it can be answered from a phone without opening
  anything.
- Roughly ninety minutes to produce once the template exists. If it is taking
  three hours, the fields are too long.

---

## Three rules that define the document

**1. It contains no picture of the website.**
A mockup here turns decision 02 into an early, unstructured Round One: the client
reviews the layout instead of agreeing the reasoning, and the Note's whole
purpose collapses. Palette and typefaces are shown because those are decisions
rendered, not layouts proposed.

**2. It is written in the client's language.**
Every noun for what she does comes from her intake. If she wrote "long-term
work", it does not become "depth-oriented practice". The test of §1 is that she
reads it and thinks *yes, that is it* — not *that is interesting*.

**3. It is not Practice Clarity.**
No personas, brand values, competitor analysis, positioning exercises,
moodboards, marketing strategy or additional discovery exercises. If a field is
pulling toward any of those, the practice may genuinely need Practice Clarity —
which is a conversation to have, not a section to add.

## The eight sections

### 0. Header — fixed

Practice name · direction chosen · date prepared · date a reply is expected ·
the words **"Direction Note · for approval"**. Nothing else. The header is where
the document announces that it is a decision rather than a report.

### 1. What I have understood — 70 words maximum

Her practice written back to her: who she works with, what she offers, how she
wants it to feel. Reflected, not reframed. This section does the single most
important job in the document, and it does it by being accurate rather than
clever.

### 2. Who the website is speaking to — 40 words maximum

One sentence naming the person arriving, and one line on what they need to find
first. Not a persona — a description of somebody in a specific state at a
specific moment.

### 3. The direction, and why it fits — 45 words maximum

Name the direction she chose and give two sentences on why it suits this
practice. If it does not suit it, this is where to say so and propose the
alternative — before anything is built to the wrong one.

### 4. How it is being tailored — 4 to 6 lines

The substance of the document. Each line is one decision plus one clause of
reasoning. Draw from: palette · typography and wordmark · page rhythm · imagery ·
the enquiry moment. Use only the ones that genuinely moved — a line reading
"typography unchanged" is filler.

### 5. Palette and typefaces — visual

A swatch band and the two faces set at real size. No moodboard, no reference
images, no mood words.

### 6. Site structure — five pages maximum

The pages in menu order, one clause each on what the page is for. Matches the
agreed scope exactly, which makes this a quiet second confirmation of what she is
buying.

### 7. What I am deliberately not doing — 2 to 3 lines

The most valuable section, and the first that will be cut in a busy week. It
prevents the "why is there no blog / no testimonials / no booking system"
conversation at Round Two, and it demonstrates judgement rather than compliance —
which is most of what £995 is buying.

### 8. Your decision — fixed

One action, unambiguous, with the consequence of each answer and the dates that
follow. Reply "approved" and the build starts on a named date, with an indicative
launch window; or send one message of changes, one revision included.

Say what this moment *is* rather than what it saves: this is the point at which
the direction can change, and nothing is built until the reply arrives. Avoid
cost-of-delay framing — "cheaper now than later" puts a price on the client
thinking carefully, which is the opposite of what the note is for.

## Reference implementation — Ashfield Counselling

Ashfield Counselling is the example practice already used in the tailoring
exhibit on the home page, taking direction 01, Rowan Hill. It is fiction, the
practitioner is deliberately unnamed, and the four annotations on that exhibit
are exactly the output of §4 — so this Note is the written half of an asset that
already exists. Palette values are illustrative.

---

**ASHFIELD COUNSELLING**
Direction Note · for approval
Direction 01 · Rowan Hill — Prepared 27 August 2026 — Reply by 3 September

**What I have understood**
You work with adults on trauma and on the kind of difficulty that does not
resolve in six sessions. Most of your clients stay a long time, and several have
tried short-term work first and found it did not hold them. You want the website
to be honest that this is slow work, without making it sound daunting.

**Who the website is speaking to**
An adult who has already had some therapy, is considering starting again, and is
quietly worried about being handed another short course. She needs to know how
you work and what it costs before she needs anything else.

**The direction, and why it fits**
Rowan Hill is the most unhurried direction in the collection, and its pace is
doing real work here rather than decoration. Nothing on the page hurries the
reader, which is the same promise your practice makes.

**How it is being tailored**

- *Palette.* Rowan Hill's warm greens give way to a cooler, steadier set —
  warmth reads as encouragement, and encouragement is the wrong note for work
  measured in years.
- *Wordmark.* The serif wordmark becomes a spaced sans and the headline drops a
  size, so a longer, plainer sentence can carry the page without shouting.
- *Page rhythm.* The three-panel strip below the hero is replaced by fees and
  availability, because your reader needs the cost before she needs anything
  else.
- *Words.* Taken from your intake. "Begin here" becomes what she is actually
  looking for, not what a website usually says.
- *Imagery.* One photograph of you and one of the room. No stock imagery.

**Palette** — `#F0EFEA` · `#1E2B2E` · `#6E7B7E` · `#8C7A6B`
**Typefaces** — Newsreader for the headline, Instrument Sans for the wordmark
and navigation

**Site structure**

- **Home** — what you do, who it is for, what it costs.
- **How I work** — the long-term work explained once, properly.
- **About** — you, your training, why this work.
- **Fees and availability** — plainly, with the waiting position.
- **Contact** — one route, low demand, no fields you do not need.

**What I am deliberately not doing**

- No list of conditions treated. It invites people to check whether they
  qualify, which is the opposite of what your first page should do.
- No online booking. A first appointment for this work should be arranged by a
  person.
- No blog. You have not said you want to write, and an empty one ages badly.

**Your decision**
Reply "approved" and I start the build on 4 September, with the first version to
you in the week of the 15th.

If something here is wrong, send it in one message and I will revise this note —
one revision is included. This is the point at which the direction can change:
nothing is built until you have replied.

---

## The test

If she read only this page and nothing else, would she know what she is getting,
feel understood, and be able to answer in one word?
