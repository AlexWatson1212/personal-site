What is collected, and why

1. Enquiries

If you use the enquiry form or email me, you provide your name, email address and whatever you choose to tell me about your practice, including your answers to the enquiry form's short questions about your work, your domain name, your photographs and your timing. The enquiry form on this website prepares a message in your own email application; it does not transmit or store anything on this website. I use enquiries to reply to you and to provide information you have asked for.

Lawful basis: your consent, and my legitimate interest in responding to people who contact my business.

2. Payment

There is no checkout on this website, and no card payment of any kind. When you have agreed the scope and the price for a Practice Identity & Website in writing, I send you an invoice and you pay it by bank transfer.

  • To raise the invoice I ask you for the name it should be made out to, a billing address, and an email address to send it to. That is all the billing information I hold.
  • No card details are involved at any point. None are entered on this website, none are sent to me, and I never see or store any.
  • The transfer itself passes between your bank and the Studio's bank. Your bank and mine each hold the information a payment carries — your name, the amount, the date and the reference — as independent controllers under their own regulatory obligations and their own privacy notices, not on my instructions.
  • I keep the invoice, and the record of what was paid and when, as a business record. Everything else I hold about your project came from you directly, by email or through the Practice Discovery questionnaire.

Lawful basis: performance of our contract; and my legal obligation to keep accurate financial records.

3. The Practice Discovery questionnaire

Once your place is reserved, I send you a link to Practice Discovery, a questionnaire on this website. It collects the written answers you choose to give about your practice and your work. Most questions are optional; nine questions are required, together with the final confirmation before submission.

The questionnaire submits the answers you enter. Netlify may also process ordinary technical information, such as an IP address, when operating and securing the service, as described under hosting below.

No files are uploaded through the questionnaire. It has no upload field of any kind. If you want me to see a logo, photographs or documents, you share a folder link — Google Drive, Dropbox or WeTransfer — or email the files to me. Files sent that way are handled by whichever email or file-sharing provider you choose for that transfer, under that provider’s own terms, and they do not pass through Netlify Forms. The sharing link itself is part of your written response, so Netlify does store the link.

The questionnaire is built into this website, and the answers you submit are received and stored by Netlify, Inc., which hosts this site and operates its form service as my processor — on my instructions, and for no purpose of its own. {% include legal-fact.html value=site.data.legal.data_protection.intake_provider todo="Confirm that Netlify's Data Processing Addendum has been read and applies to this account, and record the plan and the account name." %} Netlify, Inc. is based in the United States. Your Practice Discovery answers may therefore be processed outside the United Kingdom.

Netlify’s Data Processing Addendum, which forms part of its subscription terms, governs that processing and sets out the transfer arrangements in a defined order. Where the UK Extension to the EU–US Data Privacy Framework applies, the transfer takes place under that Extension. If it is unavailable, invalid or Netlify fails to re-certify, Netlify’s Data Processing Addendum provides for the Standard Contractual Clauses amended by the UK International Data Transfer Addendum. Netlify sets out what it does in its own words in its privacy notice and its data processing documents. ⚖

I use your answers only to create and support your Practice Fundamentals and website, and I copy them into my own project files. The questionnaire asks about your practice and your work, not your health. Please do not share information about your own health unless you want it taken into account, and do not send client names, session notes, case material or anything else about the people you work with — not in the questionnaire, not in a folder you share with me, and not by email.

Cookies and analytics

{%- if site.analytics.ga4_measurement_id != "" %}

This website sets no cookies of its own, and uses no advertising and no cross-site tracking, on any page. It offers one optional thing: Google Analytics.

Why. I am one person deciding what to write next and where to spend my time. Analytics tells me which pages people actually read, how long the longer articles hold somebody, and whether visitors arrive from a search, a directory or a link somebody sent. Without it I am guessing.

What is measured. Pages viewed and the order they are viewed in, roughly how long a page is open, the approximate part of the country or the world a visit comes from, and the kind of device and browser. Google is not given your name, your email address or your precise location, and nothing you type on this site is ever measured — not the enquiry form, not the Practice Discovery questionnaire, not a single field. Google Analytics is used for measurement only. It is not connected to any advertising product, no audience or remarketing list is built from it, and nothing about you is sold or shared for marketing.

It is optional, and it is off until you say otherwise. On your first visit a short notice asks whether you are willing. Until you answer it, and forever if you answer no, this site requests nothing from Google, sets no analytics cookie and reports no visit. Nothing about the site works differently either way, and there is nothing you cannot read or do because you declined.

If you allow it, Google Analytics sets cookies in your browser (their names begin _ga) which let it recognise a repeat visit without knowing who you are. They last up to two years. Your answer itself is not a cookie: it is a single word kept in your browser's local storage, so that you are asked once rather than on every page.

Changing your mind. At the bottom of every page, beside the Privacy and Terms links, there is a small control showing your current choice. Select it to change your answer at any time. Turning analytics off also deletes the Google Analytics cookies already in your browser, so declining removes what allowing created rather than only stopping more of it. Clearing your browser's site data has the same effect, and means you will be asked again on your next visit.

Google Analytics is provided by Google, who process this information on my behalf and may do so outside the UK. Their own explanation of what the service collects is published at policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites.

This website sets no cookies of its own and uses no analytics, advertising or tracking, on any page. If that ever changes, this notice will be updated before any such system is introduced, and consent will be sought where it is required.
