Revisions

Two consolidated rounds, shared by words and design.

At each round you review the website as a whole and send one complete, prioritised list. Copy changes and design changes share the same two rounds. Feedback sent as a stream of separate messages is held and treated as one round when it is complete.

The Direction Note is separate and comes first. One consolidated revision of the note is included, and it does not use up one of the two website rounds — the point of the note is that the direction is settled before there is a built website to change.

Putting right something that does not match a direction you have already approved is a correction, not a revision round, and does not use one of the two. You are not spending a round on my mistakes.

A round does not include a new brief, a different design or a different structural direction; additional pages; rework caused by information supplied late or changed after written approval; or features outside the chosen design system.

Practice Clarity

Included, and not something you have to ask for.

Before anything is designed, the project settles who the practice is for, what is being offered, what needs saying and what should stop being said. How much work that takes varies: for a practice you can already describe it is mostly listening and editing; for one that is still settling it takes longer. Either way it is in the price, and deciding how much is needed is my job rather than yours.

What you receive is the Direction Note — those decisions written down, before the build, for you to approve or change. If the work a practice needs is genuinely larger than a website project can carry, I will say so before anything starts.

Yours to keep

What you still hold when the project is over.

At handover you receive your practice identity written down in one document, together with the files it refers to: the wordmark in the formats a printer, a sign maker and a developer each need; the colour system specified for screen and for print; the typography specified, with the font files themselves where their licences permit; a letterhead, an email signature and a business card; short briefs to forward to a supplier; and a written specification of the website.

It is delivered in open formats — nothing in it requires a subscription to open, and no part of it can only be edited by this studio. The guide is supplied as a PDF and as its editable source, so it can be corrected and re-issued without me.

What is not in it is an open-ended set of designed applications. Three templates are produced. Everything else a practice might one day want printed or made — a leaflet, an appointment card, a door plaque, a worksheet — is specified in the guide precisely enough for a supplier to produce it, and producing it is outside this scope.
