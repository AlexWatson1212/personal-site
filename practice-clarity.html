Three worked examples

What it looks like when all nine are applied to three very different practices.

Sofia Marin, Maya Bennett and Daniel Mercer are fictional practices with finished concept websites. The strategic work behind each is published in full, set out so that every conclusion sits beside the design decision it belongs to — which is the only honest way to show that the thinking does any work. The three sites have almost nothing in common, which is rather the point.

Sixteen pages, free to read, and set out as the document a practice reviews before any design work: it settles how the practice should be described, then turns that into creative direction. Every page ends by naming what it rests on — what was established, what is being interpreted, and what is being recommended.

Read the Sofia Marin case →

Seventeen pages, and the same discipline applied to a practice with an entirely different argument: that people invent preconditions before counselling feels legitimate, and that whatever version somebody has now is enough to begin with.

Read the Maya Bennett case →

Sixteen pages, and the same discipline applied to a practice whose argument is about the threshold: that the reader has decided he would be bad at therapy, and that the site’s job is to remove the entry requirement rather than promise a result. It asks the design to carry no atmosphere at all, so that everything the visitor is meant to feel arrives as information.

Read the Daniel Mercer case →

These are portfolio case studies. A client receives the same kind of document, written for their practice and agreed before anything is designed. The three shown here were written after their concept sites, in the form a client’s is written before one.
