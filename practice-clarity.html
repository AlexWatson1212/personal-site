Six worked examples

What it looks like when all nine are applied to six very different practices.

Sofia Marin, Maya Bennett, Daniel Mercer, Helen Calder, Harbour and Stillpoint are fictional practices with finished concept websites. The strategic work behind each is published in full, set out so that every conclusion sits beside the design decision it produced — which is the only honest way to show that the thinking did any work. The six sites have almost nothing in common, which is rather the point.

Sixteen pages, free to read, and written before any design work: it settles how the practice should be described, then turns that into creative direction. Every page ends by naming what it rests on — what was established, what is being interpreted, and what is being recommended.

Read the Sofia Marin case →

Seventeen pages, and the same discipline applied to a practice with an entirely different argument: that people invent preconditions before counselling feels legitimate, and that whatever version somebody has now is enough to begin with.

Read the Maya Bennett case →

Sixteen pages, and the same discipline applied to a practice whose argument is about the threshold: that the reader has decided he would be bad at therapy, and that the site’s job is to remove the entry requirement rather than promise a result. It asks the design to carry no atmosphere at all, so that everything the visitor is meant to feel arrives as information.

Read the Daniel Mercer case →

Sixteen pages, and the same discipline applied to a practice whose argument is about role: that the reader has already decided to have therapy and stopped at choosing a person, so the document has to establish what this particular therapist contributes without taking authority over the client’s conclusion. It specifies that as a typographic pair — the reader’s material, then the contribution entering and stopping.

Read the Helen Calder case →

Nineteen pages, and the one document that had to prove the process transfers from a person to an organisation. It has an organisational identity section rather than a professional one, four readerships over two pages instead of a single ideal client, a page about a building, and an eight-stage sequence setting out what each stage has to settle.

Read the Harbour case →

Seventeen pages — the length the argument needed rather than a fixed count. It is the one document that specifies the finished site page by page: the website translation runs to a second sheet giving the pages, the order of the homepage, what belongs in the first screen, and what the mobile layout must not do.

Read the Stillpoint case →
