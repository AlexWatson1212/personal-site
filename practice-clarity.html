Six worked examples

What it looks like when all nine are applied to six very different practices.

Sofia Marin, Maya Bennett, Daniel Mercer, Helen Calder, Harbour and Stillpoint are fictional practices with finished concept websites. The strategic work behind each is published in full, set out so that every conclusion sits beside the design decision it produced — which is the only honest way to show that the thinking did any work. The six sites have almost nothing in common, which is rather the point.

Fourteen pages, free to read, and honest about its own order: the website existed first, and the document reconstructs the foundation that could credibly have produced it. It separates what the site demonstrably does from what that suggests about the practice behind it.

Read the Sofia Marin case →

Thirteen pages, and the same discipline applied to a practice with an entirely different argument: that people invent preconditions before counselling feels legitimate, and that the unprepared account is the more useful material.

Read the Maya Bennett case →

Twenty-four pages, and the same discipline applied to a practice whose argument is about the threshold: that the reader has decided he would be bad at therapy, and that the site’s job is to remove the entry requirement rather than promise a result. Every page is marked as evidence or as inference, and one names the inferences that could be wrong.

Read the Daniel Mercer case →

Fourteen pages, and the same discipline applied to a practice whose argument is about role: that the reader has already decided to have therapy and stopped at choosing a person, so the document has to establish what this particular therapist contributes without taking authority over the client’s conclusion. One page is given over to what the practice is not.

Read the Helen Calder case →

Fourteen pages, and the one document that had to prove the process transfers from a person to an organisation. It has an organisational identity section rather than a professional one, four readerships over two pages instead of a single ideal client, and a page about a building. A late page names the four places where it is inferring hardest.

Read the Harbour case →

Thirteen pages — the length the argument needed rather than a fixed count. Thirty quotations are set verbatim and attributed to the page they appear on, every conclusion drawn on top of them carries an inference mark, and each translation row was checked against the finished build. Where a row and the site disagreed, the site changed, and the closing page says so.
