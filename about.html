The business behind the work

Built around what I am actually good at.

I work best when I can take something substantial, understand what makes it work and refine it independently. Starting every project from a blank page added uncertainty without automatically adding value — so the collection became the default, and the blank page became a deliberate choice rather than a habit.

That is why there are two routes rather than one. Most practices are better served by a strong direction, tailored properly, at a price published in advance. Some need the thinking done first, and an original design that comes out of it. Both get the same standard of craft.

Less uncertainty for the client. More focused craft from me. No reduction in quality.

What guides the Studio

Professional does not have to mean generic.

A therapy website has practical work to do, but it also creates a room in the visitor's mind. Good design makes the practice easier to recognise without making claims the therapist cannot honestly hold.

01

Let people feel it

A person often knows whether a website feels safe, clear or credible before they can explain why. The visual atmosphere matters.

02

Make the next step plain

Fees, location, suitability and the enquiry route should not be hidden behind branding language.

03

Do not overstate

The website should make the real quality of a practice visible, not manufacture a grander version of it.

04

Keep the process contained

Written questions, clear approval points and consolidated revisions make creative work calmer for everyone.

Website craft

I notice hierarchy, rhythm and the point where a visitor gets lost.

I care about typography, responsive behaviour, accessibility, performance and search fundamentals—but I use them in service of a clear human experience.

Counselling understanding

I know that trust is not produced by soft colours and the word “safe.”

My training helps me question generic therapeutic language, notice the emotional demand a page places on someone, and treat the enquiry journey with care.

What it is like to work with me

Thoughtful, direct and mostly written.

Written communication is the default. It gives both of us time to think, keeps decisions in one place and reduces meetings that do not improve the work.

Communication
Clear written questions and considered recommendations. Calls only where genuinely useful.
Capacity
A small number of active projects so the work receives focused attention.
Feedback
Two complete, consolidated rounds rather than messages arriving one change at a time.
Honesty
If the lower-cost route fits, I will recommend it. If something needs specialist support, I will say so.
