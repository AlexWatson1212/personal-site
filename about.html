About

I make websites, and I understand the work they have to hold.

Years in marketing, personal branding and creating content online; now training as a counsellor in Greater Manchester. The Studio brings those two things together for therapists and counsellors.

Why this, and why me

Two worlds, brought together.

Before counselling, I spent years working in marketing and personal branding and creating content online, including building my own audience on YouTube. That taught me what it takes to communicate a person clearly in public — and what it is like to want to do that without performing or selling.

Since moving into counselling I have noticed that therapist websites usually start with the website: what it should look like and what the homepage should say. I think that is starting too late. So I start with the practice — who you work with, how you work, what matters to you, and what somebody needs to understand before they contact you. I write that down as your Practice Fundamentals, and the website is built from it.

It should not look like my idea of a good therapist website. It should look like your practice.

Two kinds of attention

Professional does not have to mean generic.

Website craft

I notice hierarchy, rhythm and the point where a visitor gets lost.

Typography, responsive behaviour, accessibility, performance and search fundamentals — used in service of a clear human experience rather than as a list of features.

Counselling training

Trust is not produced by soft colours and the word “safe”.

Level 4 training and supervised client work help me question generic therapeutic language, notice the emotional demand a page places on somebody, and treat first contact as a real moment rather than a step in a process.

The training is not a design credential, and it is not a marketing one either. It gives me useful context for the work these websites have to hold, and that is the whole of the claim.

A related question people ask me often: how much should you say about yourself on a therapy website?

What it is like to work with me

Thoughtful, direct and mostly written.

Written communication is the default. It gives both of us time to think, keeps decisions in one place, and removes meetings that do not improve the work.

Communication
Clear written questions and considered recommendations. Calls only where they genuinely help.
Capacity
A small number of active projects, so the work gets focused attention.
Feedback
Two consolidated feedback stages — one on the direction, one on the built website — rather than messages arriving one change at a time.
Honesty
If I am not the right person, or something needs specialist support, I say so.
